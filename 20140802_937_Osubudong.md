## The Nature of Viruses and Human Vulnerability 
Huh Kyung young began by discussing the Ebola virus, noting its origin in Africa and its classification by international health organizations. He clarified that Ebola is a virus, not a bacterium, and emphasized that viruses are microscopic and invisible to the human eye, often airborne. He equated viruses to genetic material, explaining that they can destroy human genes, leading to death. This vulnerability highlights a fundamental aspect of human existence: despite our perceived strength, humanity remains susceptible to unseen forces like viruses, for which no universal cure exists. 


## The Analogy of "Sotae" and Human Behavior 
Huh Kyung young drew an analogy to a traditional method of weaning children by applying a bitter substance called "sotae" to the mother's breast. Just as a child, once tasting the bitterness, refrains from further nursing, humans, too, can be deterred from harmful behaviors by appropriate "stimuli." He posited that from a divine perspective, humans are like children who often disregard verbal warnings. However, when confronted with a "sotae-like" consequence, they cease undesirable actions. This concept extends to the "three poisons" (탐진치 - greed, anger, and ignorance) that plague humanity. Even spiritual teachings from figures like Buddha or Jesus often fail to curb these inherent human tendencies. Huh Kyung young asserted that those who follow his teachings and view his image will be protected from future epidemics, including new viruses like the child influenza virus. 


## The Principle of Osubudong (Five Animals in Motion) 
Huh Kyung young introduced the "Truth of Osubudong," which translates to the "Five Animals in Motion." This principle illustrates a natural hierarchy of fear and power:

- **Mouse** fears the **Cat**. 
- **Cat** fears the **Dog**. 
- **Dog** fears the **Tiger**. 
- **Tiger** fears the **Elephant**. 
- **Elephant** fears the **Mouse**. 
This cyclical relationship demonstrates that there is no eternal strongman in nature; power constantly shifts. This natural law, according to Huh Kyung young, mirrors human society. 


## The Cycle of Power in Human Society 
The Osubudong principle extends to human societal structures, illustrating that even the most powerful individuals are ultimately subject to the collective will.

- **Human sentiment** cannot overcome **reason**. 
- **Reason** cannot overcome **law**. 
- **Law** cannot overcome **power**. 
- **Power** (e.g., a judge) is subservient to **higher authority** (e.g., a president). 
- **Presidents** are subservient to **Heaven**. 
- **Heaven** ultimately fears the **people (백성)**. 
This means that even a president, despite their immense power, is ultimately accountable to the citizens. Just as a family's children can influence a father's decisions, the collective will of the people can bring down even the most powerful leader. 


## The Three Sovereign Rights of the People 
Huh Kyung young emphasized that individuals should not envy those in power, as the people themselves possess ultimate authority. He outlined the three fundamental sovereign rights guaranteed by the constitution:
1. **Right to Elect (선출권)**: The power to choose leaders, such as the president. 
2. **Right to Resist (저항권)**: The right to oppose unsatisfactory governance. 
3. **Right to Revolution (혁명권)**: The ultimate power to overthrow an unsatisfactory government, as exemplified by historical events like the April 19th Revolution. 

These rights demonstrate that the people are the true masters of the nation, holding the power to change leadership at any time. This reinforces the Osubudong principle, where even the seemingly strongest entity (the elephant, or in this case, the president) can be overcome by the smallest (the mouse, or the collective people). 


## The Importance of Others-Centered Perspective 
Huh Kyung young highlighted the importance of adopting an "others-centered" perspective, using the example of an ambulance's reversed lettering. The word "AMBULANCE" is written backward on the front of the vehicle so that drivers looking in their rearview mirrors see it correctly. This illustrates that true success comes from considering the perspective and convenience of others, rather than solely one's own. If one only seeks personal convenience, success is unattainable. 


## The Accumulation of Sins and Virtues 
Huh Kyung young recounted a parable about a person who dies and faces judgment.

- First, they are given a book detailing their **sins of action**, which fills one volume. 
- Next, they receive 100 volumes for their **sins of speech**. 
- Finally, they are presented with 10,000 volumes for their **sins of thought**. 
- In stark contrast, their **good deeds** are recorded on a single sheet of paper. 
This parable underscores the vast disparity between human transgressions and good deeds, emphasizing the ease with which one accumulates negative karma through actions, words, and thoughts. 


## The Path to Happiness and Escaping the Cycle 
To avoid a negative afterlife and achieve happiness, Huh Kyung young stressed the importance of benefiting others rather than focusing solely on oneself. This principle, known as "자이이타 (Jari-ita)," where one benefits oneself by benefiting others, leads to true happiness and ensures the well-being of one's family. He claimed that those who listen to his lectures and see his face multiple times will have their "energy changed to zero," implying a cleansing of past sins and a fresh start. 


## The Wisdom of Ancient Sayings and the Nature of Good and Evil 
Huh Kyung young referenced ancient wisdom to further illustrate the consequences of good and bad deeds:

- **"Even a single good deed, though its reward may not yet be visible, automatically distances misfortune."** 
- Conversely, **"Even a single bad deed, though its harm may not be immediately apparent, automatically causes blessings to flee."** 
He then cited a saying comparing good deeds to "grass on a spring mountain," which grows imperceptibly but steadily flourishes, leading to continuous blessings. Bad deeds, however, are like "sharpening a knife on a rough stone," where the blade appears unchanged initially but gradually dulls, leading to continuous decline. 


## The Distinction Between "Gentleman" and "Playboy" 
Huh Kyung young explained the difference between a "gentleman" and a "playboy" through their underlying intentions. A gentleman is someone who considers and serves others, always putting others first. A playboy, however, is self-centered, prioritizing their own desires and pleasures. He concluded that a playboy's soul remains immature, like that of a boy, while a gentleman embodies a mature spirit. 


## Religious Syncretism and the Evolution of Faith 
Huh Kyung young delved into the interconnectedness of religions, particularly Buddhism and Christianity.

- He noted that the term "elder" (장로) appears frequently in Buddhist scriptures and was adopted into Christianity during translation, while the term "pastor" (목사) is not found in the Bible. He suggested that "pastor" refers to someone living without purpose, while "elder" signifies someone dedicated to religious contribution. 
- He highlighted that the Buddhist text, the Avatamsaka Sutra (화엄경), is more profound and expansive than the Bible, particularly in its cosmological views. Buddhism, 2500 years ago, spoke of "three thousand great thousand worlds" (삼천대천세계), implying an infinite number of stars, a concept now being confirmed by modern science. In contrast, the Bible, at the time of its writing, described a flat Earth with only the sun, moon, and Earth. 
- He presented research by Professor Min Hee-sik of Hanyang University, a Christian scholar who received a French cultural grand medal for his study of Jesus's life. This research, aired on KBS, revealed that Jesus traveled to India at age 13 and studied under a guru named Mendella. 
- Huh Kyung young also pointed out the linguistic connection between "Amen" in Christianity and "Om" (Aum) in Buddhism, suggesting a shared root in ancient Indian languages. 
- He further asserted that many European churches were originally "temples" (사원) in the Buddhist sense, later rebranded as "churches" (교회) by Protestantism. He noted similarities in practices like bell-ringing and the use of wooden fish (목어) and rosaries (염주) in both traditions. 
These points underscore Huh Kyung young's belief that all religions share a common origin and that religious conflicts are ultimately baseless. 


## The Future of Global Conflict and Huh Kyung young's Role 
Huh Kyung young predicted that religious wars, such as those between Judaism and Islam, would escalate in the West, leading to a potential World War III. He also warned that major powers like the United States, China, Russia, and Japan were eyeing the Korean Peninsula as a potential battleground. He stated that his presence on Earth is to prevent such conflicts, asserting that he will unify the world and end religious strife. 


## Huh Kyung young's Presidential Inauguration Vision 
Huh Kyung young detailed his unconventional vision for his presidential inauguration, which he believes will unify the world and establish Korea's global leadership.

- **Location**: Not at the National Assembly, which represents only the legislative branch, but at Gwanghwamun Plaza, Seoul City Hall Plaza, or Seoul Station Plaza, where millions of citizens can gather. 
- **Arrival**: He envisions descending from the sky like a hang glider with angel wings, accompanied by 12 angels, circling above the crowd before landing on a stage. This dramatic entrance, he claims, will be a global spectacle, demonstrating his divine authority and setting a new precedent for leadership. 
- **Procession**: Upon landing, the 12 angels will vanish into a mist, and Huh Kyung young will mount a large white horse, leading a procession of 33 white horses ridden by 33 individuals. The procession will be led by a person carrying the flag of Gojoseon (ancient Korea), symbolizing Korea's historical prominence. His own cape will be uniquely colored and dramatically long, flowing 5 meters behind him as he rides. 
- **Stage Setup**: The inauguration stage will be at the same height as the audience, emphasizing equality between the president and the people. No high-ranking officials will sit on an elevated platform; instead, they will be seated among the citizens. The stage itself will be used for musical performances by top Korean artists, creating a festive atmosphere rather than a formal ceremony. 
- **Purpose**: This elaborate "performance," meticulously planned like a movie script, is designed to capture global attention and establish Huh Kyung young's unique authority, demonstrating that he is not an ordinary leader but one descended from the heavens. He believes this will assert the "spirit of the Korean people" and reclaim Korea's historical influence over vast territories. 

## The Paradox of the Outcast Becoming King 
Huh Kyung young presented the idea that those who are initially "outcasts" (왕따) often rise to become "kings" or leaders. He cited several examples:

- **Bill Clinton**: Faced a difficult childhood with multiple stepfathers, leading to a sense of being an outcast. 
- **Barack Obama**: Experienced discrimination as a person of color, leading to isolation and even drug use before his recovery and rise to the presidency. 
- **Napoleon**: Was ridiculed for his short stature, which fueled his ambition to become emperor. 
- **President Park Chung-hee**: Suffered from poverty and a small stature, motivating him to develop Korea's economy. 
- **Successful individuals**: Those who achieve great success, such as passing the bar exam, often do so because they lack social distractions and material comforts in their youth, forcing them to focus on their studies. 
- **Historical figures**: Scientists like Galileo and Copernicus, who challenged the geocentric view, were initially persecuted as outcasts but were later recognized for their genius. Jesus, too, was an outcast who was crucified, a deeply humiliating Roman form of execution, rather than the Jewish method of stoning. 
Huh Kyung young concluded that he himself has been an outcast, but this very experience is the beginning of his path to becoming a leader. He asserted that as science advances, it will only further reveal the limitations of human understanding and the inexplicable nature of his abilities, which scientists are now beginning to study. 


## The Limitations of Science and the Divine Order 
Huh Kyung young argued that science, despite its advancements, is ultimately limited in its ability to comprehend the mysteries of the universe and certain phenomena.

- **Cosmic Mystery**: As telescopes improve, scientists become more bewildered by the infinite and complex nature of the cosmos, leading some to admit their inability to fully explain it. 
- **Huh Kyung young's Abilities**: He claimed that his abilities to change milk and water, heal people, and manipulate genes are beyond scientific explanation. He mentioned that Dr. Jeon Se-il, director of CHA University's College of Medicine, is currently researching his abilities to cure illnesses, which he demonstrated by healing 10 patients brought by doctors. 
- **Traditional Fermentation**: He also cited traditional fermentation processes, like making doenjang (soybean paste) and ganjang (soy sauce), as examples of phenomena that scientists cannot fully explain. These processes, he stated, rely on the "instructions of Heaven" given to microorganisms. He believes that the taste of these fermented foods can even predict a family's fortune, with a change in taste signaling impending ruin. This highlights a divine order that governs even seemingly mundane aspects of life.
