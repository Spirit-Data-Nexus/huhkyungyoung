## Content


## The Philosophical Concepts of Gong, Mu, and Hwan 
Huh Kyung young introduces the profound philosophical concepts of Gong (공), Mu (무), and Hwan (환), emphasizing their critical importance for understanding the true nature of existence. He challenges the audience to articulate these concepts visually and verbally, asserting that a deep comprehension of them surpasses the knowledge of even a philosophy Ph.D. The inability of many to define these terms, especially Gong, leads to a lifetime of misunderstanding and a failure to grasp the fundamental lessons intended for human existence.


## Differentiating Gong from Space and Nothingness 
Huh Kyung young clarifies that Gong is often mistakenly equated with mere space or nothingness. He explains that a three-dimensional object, like a soccer ball, represents space (공간) and dimension (차원), but not true Gong. This spatial understanding, prevalent in some Buddhist interpretations, is merely a superficial aspect. True Gong transcends physical dimensions and is not simply an empty volume.


## The Misconception of Gong, Mu, and Hwan 
Many individuals live their entire lives in confusion, conflating Gong, Mu, and Hwan. Huh Kyung young stresses that human existence on Earth is a journey of learning, and failing to acquire this knowledge diminishes one's spiritual level in the afterlife. Accumulating wealth without spiritual understanding leads to a lower state of being, underscoring the necessity of philosophical study.


## Defining Mu (Nothingness) 
Mu (무) is defined as absolute nothingness, devoid of any inner or outer surface, or even a point. It is smaller than anything conceivable in the universe because even the smallest particle, like a sperm cell, possesses an exterior and interior. Mu is therefore easy to express conceptually as "non-existence" itself, requiring no visual representation. It is not merely something that disappears by burning; rather, it is the complete absence of form and substance.


## The True Nature of Gong (Emptiness) 
Huh Kyung young illustrates Gong through an analogy involving two observers, A and B, a tree, and a mirror. Observer A sees the tree directly, while Observer B sees the tree reflected in the mirror. However, if Observer A is absent, the tree, as perceived by B, ceases to exist. This phenomenon, where an object's existence is contingent upon an observer, is what Huh Kyung young defines as Gong. He relates this to quantum theory, describing it as "inter-theory" (사이 이론), where the space between observer and observed is Gong.


## Gong as Illusion and Shadow 
Gong is further explained as an illusion or shadow, not a true entity. Just as the moon appears differently to various observers, its perceived form is not its ultimate reality. This variability in perception highlights that Gong is a reflection, a "fake" reality, rather than a concrete substance.


## Money as Gong 
Huh Kyung young extends the concept of Gong to money. Money, though seemingly real, is fundamentally Gong. It is merely paper with an imprinted image, its value contingent on human perception and utility. For an elderly person on their deathbed, money holds no value; it becomes "trash" because they lack the capacity to use it. This demonstrates that money's perceived reality is an illusion, a form of Gong, rather than an absolute truth.


## Earth and Possessions as Gong 
The Earth itself and all material possessions are also forms of Gong. For someone facing imminent death, the Earth holds no personal significance. Similarly, a grand estate becomes Gong, an illusion, because its value is entirely dependent on the observer's perspective and needs. Understanding this concept of Gong is crucial for a proper worldview.


## The Distinction Between Gong and Mu 
It is vital to distinguish Gong from Mu. Mu signifies complete annihilation, where something ceases to exist entirely. Gong, however, refers to a situation where a physical object exists, but its presence or absence is determined by the observer's perception or need. The object itself remains, but its reality is fluid.


## The Pervasiveness of Gong in Life 
Once one truly comprehends Gong, everything in the world appears as such. Material objects, human popularity, and even revolutionary movements are ultimately fleeting and impermanent. People strive and fight for things that are ultimately Gong, bound by these illusions.


## Human Relationships and Possessions as Gong 
Even deeply personal relationships and possessions, such as a child, can be understood as Gong. A child, viewed differently by a mother-in-law and a wife, exists as a "virtual world" for each. The child's true self remains unknown, and the emotional attachment to them, while strong, is ultimately an attachment to an illusion. Children may even anticipate inheriting wealth, making the parental bond a form of attachment to something ultimately empty.


## The Futility of Human Endeavors 
Human endeavors, like building the Tower of Babel or engaging in "empty prayers" (공염불), are ultimately Gong. They are like structures built with wind, destined to collapse. The only true and lasting knowledge is the truth that transcends lifetimes and determines one's spiritual level in the afterlife.


## The Afterlife and Reincarnation 
Huh Kyung young explains that after death, individuals are tested based on their life's actions, which determines their spiritual level and destination. Those who fail to understand these truths are simply "pushed aside" in the afterlife.


## Earth as Heaven and Hell 
Heaven and hell are not separate realms but exist on Earth. Individuals who commit sins on Earth are punished on Earth, just as a criminal in a specific province is incarcerated in that province's prison. There is no escape to other planets or galaxies for punishment.


## Reincarnation of the Wicked 
Those who commit grave sins, such as usurers who exploit the poor, do not qualify for a human rebirth in a better place like Africa. Instead, they are reborn as animals, such as earthworms or mosquitoes, to suffer the consequences of their actions. A mosquito, for instance, risks its life for every drop of blood, experiencing constant fear and danger, mirroring the exploitation it inflicted in its human life.


## The Fate of Usurers 
Huh Kyung young asserts that wicked usurers are 100% guaranteed to be reborn as mosquitoes, constantly seeking to extract "blood" (money) from others. However, legitimate banks, which provide necessary capital and charge reasonable interest, are not considered wicked. Those who exploit others' wealth are highly likely to become mosquitoes.


## The Importance of Righteous Living 
Understanding these consequences should deter people from committing sins. It encourages choosing a path of moderate, ethical work rather than ruthless exploitation. Every action has a precise consequence, leaving no room for error, thus limiting human freedom to act without accountability.


## The Privilege of Human Birth 
Being born as a human, especially in a place like Africa, is considered a blessing, indicating good karma from past lives. It is difficult to be born in paradise, even more difficult to be born human, and traditionally, even more challenging to be born male.


## Historical Misrepresentation of Women 
Huh Kyung young criticizes historical practices, such as excluding women from population counts and voting rights, as seen in Europe until relatively recently. He also points out inaccuracies in religious texts, like the Bible's mention of "5,000 people" or "2 million people" crossing the Red Sea, suggesting these numbers are exaggerated and do not account for the logistical impossibilities. He emphasizes that such figures are often symbolic rather than literal.


## The Illusion of Perception 
Humans are often trapped in a world of illusion, unable to see the true reality. They perceive others only from their own limited perspective, leading to judgments of good and evil. This is why one should not "eat from the tree of knowledge of good and evil," as it leads to misjudgment.


## Defining Hwan (Illusion/Dream) 
Hwan (환) is defined as illusion or dream. Huh Kyung young refers to the Cheonbu-gyeong (천부경), specifically the sixth line, which speaks of "Unsam, Sa, Seonghwan, O, Chil, I, I" (운삼사성환오칠이이), to explain Hwan. He states that Hwan is what appears in dreams.


## Hwan as the Intersection of Heaven and Earth 
Hwan is visually represented as the intersection (교집합) between Heaven (체, spirit/soul) and Earth (용, mind). It is the overlapping area where the soul and mind meet. This intermediate realm is where dreams occur. Dreams are not part of the physical Earth nor the spiritual Heaven but exist in this overlapping space.


## Hwan as Dream, Bubble, Shadow, Dew, and Lightning 
Hwan is likened to a dream, a water bubble, a shadow, dew, and lightning. These metaphors emphasize its ephemeral and transient nature. Life itself is a fleeting illusion, passing quickly like a dream or a flash of lightning.


## The Illusion of Attachment 
Humans often mistake these transient illusions for reality and become attached to them. This attachment to Hwan, or fantasy, is a fundamental human flaw.


## Che (Body/Substance) and Yong (Function/Desire) 
Huh Kyung young explains the concepts of Che (체) and Yong (용). Che, derived from the character for "body" (몸체), represents the fundamental essence or "본체" (original body). He equates this fundamental essence of humanity with "God" (신). Humans, however, often fail to see this true essence and instead focus on the "shadow" (그림자), which is Yong. Yong, in turn, is equated with "greed" (욕심) and "survival of the fittest" (적자생존). People live by pursuing Yong, driven by the need to survive and succeed, often resorting to any means necessary.


## The Flaw of "Survival of the Fittest" 
"Survival of the fittest" is presented as a lower-level human pursuit. Those who prioritize it, like Jesus who died for truth rather than adapting, often fail to "survive" in the worldly sense. Such individuals, driven by greed, are destined to be reborn as animals, unable to reclaim human form in the next life. Huh Kyung young asserts that he is the one who established this cosmic order.


## The Consequences of Worldly Pursuits 
Huh Kyung young laments that people, despite hearing these profound truths, continue to prioritize worldly pursuits like drinking, socializing, and career advancement, often at the expense of their spiritual growth. This leads them down a path of "survival of the fittest," abandoning their true human nature. He warns that upon death, they will realize the futility of their actions, regretting not having followed his teachings, and facing rebirth in the animal kingdom.


## Misguided Parental Sacrifices 
Parents often make sacrifices for their children, accumulating wealth and enduring hardships, only to find that their children are merely waiting for their inheritance. This misguided devotion, without spiritual "currency exchange" (환전), leaves them unprepared for the afterlife.


## Distinguishing Gu-bun (Categorization) and Bun-ri (Separation) 
Huh Kyung young differentiates between Gu-bun (구분), meaning categorization or distinction, and Bun-ri (분리), meaning separation. He uses the analogy of bibimbap: one cannot "separate" the ingredients once mixed (Bun-ri is impossible), but one can "categorize" different dishes like doenjang jjigae or bibimbap (Gu-bun is possible). People often confuse these two concepts, hindering their wisdom.


## The Origin of Hwan (Illusion) 
Hwan (illusion) arises from "Jeong-sin" (정신), which is the mental faculty residing between the soul (영혼) and the mind (마음). The mind, associated with conscious thought and immediate desires, rests, but Jeong-sin and the soul do not. It is Jeong-sin that creates dreams and illusions.


## Jin-gong (Vacuum) and Myo-yu (Subtle Existence) 
Huh Kyung young introduces the concepts of Jin-gong (진공), meaning vacuum or emptiness, and Myo-yu (묘유), meaning subtle existence or mysterious presence. The soul is close to Jin-gong, representing the realm of truth, which is empty and allows men to quickly attain enlightenment. The mind, however, is Myo-yu, a mysterious and unpredictable aspect, often associated with women's minds. These two concepts are opposites: Jin-gong is truth, while Myo-yu is not. Humans should strive for Jin-gong, but their minds often dwell in Myo-yu, constantly creating and destroying illusions. Ultimately, Jin-gong and Myo-yu are one, but humans categorize them as heaven and earth.


## The Absence of Illusion for the Enlightened 
For those who understand Jin-gong and Myo-yu as one, there is no need for illusions. Their reality is already paradise. Foolish individuals constantly chase future success and wealth, while the enlightened find peace and contentment in their present circumstances. They are grateful for their children's health and existence, having already received and given everything.


## The Pitfalls of Parental Ambition 
Excessive parental ambition, such as forcing children to become judges or doctors, leads to unhappiness for both parents and children. Such children often become estranged or even a source of pain. It is best to allow children to follow their own paths, as forcing them into roles can lead to failure and unhappiness.


## The Fate of Parents with Sons vs. Daughters 
Huh Kyung young contrasts the fates of mothers with sons and those with daughters. Mothers with sons often face neglect and are passed between their children's homes, eventually dying alone on the street. In contrast, parents with daughters are often cared for at home, dying peacefully within their own houses, as daughters tend to be more supportive.


## The Unity of Jin-gong and Myo-yu 
Despite human tendencies to categorize, Jin-gong and Myo-yu are fundamentally one.


## The Power of Huh Kyung young's Name and Energy 
Huh Kyung young asserts that merely looking at his picture or calling his name instantly boosts one's energy, unlike looking at other people's pictures or calling other names, which depletes energy. This is because the universe is composed of energy, and Huh Kyung young controls all cosmic energy.


## The Three Elements of Cancer 
Huh Kyung young identifies three key elements contributing to cancer:
1. **Qi-Heo (기허 - Energy Deficiency):** This is a lack of vital energy.
2. **Qi-Ul (기울 - Energy Stagnation):** This refers to blocked or stagnant energy, often manifesting as blood clots or tumors (e.g., breast cancer, uterine fibroids).
3. **Stress (스트레스):** This is mental and emotional strain.


## The Role of Greed, Anger, and Ignorance in Cancer 
These three elements are linked to the "three poisons" of Buddhism:
1. **Greed (탐심):** Excessive desire leads to Qi-Heo (energy deficiency). Overworking due to ambition depletes one's vital energy, leading to illness. He notes that long-lived individuals rarely exercise, as excessive exercise generates harmful reactive oxygen species, shortening life.
2. **Anger (진심):** This leads to Qi-Ul (energy stagnation).
3. **Ignorance (치심):** This leads to stress. People who are ignorant or foolish experience more stress. Athletes, for instance, often suffer from stress despite their physical prowess, indicating a lack of intellectual understanding.


## Overcoming the Three Poisons 
By understanding the concepts of Gong, Mu, and Hwan, one can overcome ignorance and eliminate stress. By refraining from anger and seeing others kindly while being critical of oneself, one can avoid the conditions that lead to cancer.


## The Autonomic Nervous System and Cancer 
Huh Kyung young explains that the autonomic nervous system, given by God, plays a role in creating cancer. The sympathetic nervous system (교감 신경), associated with stress and fight-or-flight responses, leads to cancer. In contrast, the parasympathetic nervous system (부교감 신경), associated with rest and calm, prevents cancer.


## The Path of the Learned 
Learned individuals operate under the parasympathetic nervous system, maintaining inner stability and avoiding excessive actions. They understand the impermanence of things and live without anxiety. They strive for "Sa-sa Mu-ae" (사사무애), meaning freedom from obstacles in all matters.


## Sa-sa Mu-ae: Freedom from Obstacles 
"Sa-sa Mu-ae" implies freedom from obstacles in both the material (이판) and spiritual (사판) worlds. It means having no impediments in one's actions or thoughts, leading to a clear mind and a peaceful life. This ultimate state allows one to complete their life's journey calmly, like an angel.


## The Longevity Granted by Huh Kyung young 
Calling Huh Kyung young's name can significantly extend one's lifespan.


## The Cause of Disabilities 
Disabilities are not solely the result of parental sin but are connected to the collective karma of Earth's inhabitants and the parents' actions. While some religions portray disabled individuals as "gifts from God" for educational purposes, Huh Kyung young suggests that disabilities can arise from specific circumstances during conception, such as sexual activity during a thunderstorm, on an inauspicious day, or during a shocking event, or even if someone interrupts the act.
