## 20140517\_The\_Truth\_of\_Words\_and\_Waves



## \[English Title] The Truth of Words and Waves: Understanding the Vibrational Nature of Reality



## Date: May 17, 2014



## Keywords: Vibration, Consciousness, Cycles, DNA, Spirituality



## Content



## &nbsp;The Vibrational Cycles of Existence

Huh Kyung young explains that everything in existence operates on vibrational cycles. For instance, a rat operates on a 242-cycle, meaning it can sense a ship's sinking 242 cycles in advance. Humans, however, operate on a much broader spectrum, ranging from 7 to 10,000 cycles. Children, with their pure and untainted minds, possess lower cycles, making them more sensitive to subtle vibrations, such as the sound of a cell phone. As individuals age, their cycles tend to increase, indicating a greater accumulation of worldly experiences and a potential loss of spiritual purity. This concept suggests that a person with a pure spiritual essence and a good "wave" (vibration) is someone whose cycles are low and clear.



## &nbsp;The Power of Words and Hormonal Responses

Huh Kyung young emphasizes the profound impact of words, stating that they possess immense power. He illustrates this by explaining the physiological response to negative speech. When one curses another, the brain instantly releases harmful hormones like noradrenaline and adrenaline. A single drop of these hormones can sicken every cell in the body. This rapid cellular response, occurring within 0.1 seconds, highlights how quickly negative words can affect one's physical well-being. Conversely, positive words and thoughts can have a beneficial effect, though the immediate impact of positive words on the body is not as dramatically illustrated as the negative.



## &nbsp;The Hierarchy of Existence: From Body to Spirit

Huh Kyung young outlines a hierarchical structure of existence, starting from the physical and ascending to the spiritual. He explains that hair and skin are born from the flesh, flesh from bones, and bones from blood. This establishes the physical realm. Beyond the physical, blood is born from the mind, and the mind from the spirit. The spirit, in turn, is born from the soul. This progression illustrates a deep interconnectedness, where each layer is an "mother" to the next, ultimately leading to the soul.



## The Difference Between Mind and Spirit

Huh Kyung young differentiates between the mind and the spirit using an analogy. If a mother asks her child to buy noodles, but the child gets distracted by delicious snacks in the store and forgets the noodles, this forgetfulness is attributed to the spirit. The spirit, in this context, represents the ability to hold onto a thought or purpose. However, if the child's mind is simply captivated by the snacks, without necessarily forgetting the original task, that is the mind's immediate reaction. This distinction suggests that the spirit is responsible for sustained focus and purpose, while the mind reacts to immediate stimuli.



## &nbsp;The Genesis and the Power of Sound

Huh Kyung young delves into the biblical account of creation, emphasizing the significance of "the Word" and "sound." He states that sound is a higher stage than words, and that the universe began with sound. The sequence of creation, according to him, is: God (the originator) -> Sound (God's child) -> Light and Darkness -> Firmament (space) and Water (hydrogen gas) -> Plants and Nature -> Fish, Birds, Animals, and Humans. This order highlights sound as the fundamental creative force, preceding even light. He also notes that the entire process from sound to the creation of hair takes only 0.1 seconds, demonstrating the incredible speed of divine creation.



## The Three Types of Gratitude

Huh Kyung young categorizes gratitude into three types:

1. **Conditional Gratitude:** This is the most basic form, where one expresses thanks for something received or for a favor. For example, thanking someone for a meal or for paying tuition. Huh Kyung young advises against this type of gratitude, as it is common and expected.
2. **Gratitude Despite Adversity:** This is a higher form of gratitude, where one can be thankful even when facing difficulties or being wronged. For instance, being grateful to one's mother despite her hardships, or thanking someone who has harmed you.
3. **Unconditional Gratitude:** This is the highest form, where gratitude is offered without any conditions. It is akin to "unconditional giving" (무주상보시) in Buddhism. This means being thankful even if someone steps on your foot. This level of gratitude is seen as a powerful spiritual practice.



## &nbsp;The Earth's Protective Layers and Human Responsibility

Huh Kyung young explains that the Earth is protected by various atmospheric layers, including the troposphere, ozone layer (30km above ground), stratosphere (60km), ionosphere (80km), and magnetosphere (700,000km). These layers act as a "womb" or "amniotic sac" for Earth, shielding it from harmful cosmic energies and radiation. He likens the ozone layer to a mother's amniotic fluid, protecting the fetus. However, human activities, such as pollution and war, are likened to a fetus "piercing" the amniotic sac with its fingernails, causing damage to these protective layers. This destruction, he warns, will ultimately lead to the demise of humanity.



## &nbsp;The Future of Korea: Four Major Industries

Huh Kyung young outlines four major industries that will shape Korea's future:

1. **Spiritual/Meditation Industry:** This is considered the most crucial. Korea is envisioned as a global center for spiritual enlightenment, where people from around the world come to learn truth and receive spiritual guidance. This industry is seen as a return to humanity's spiritual roots.
2. **Design Industry:** This focuses on aesthetics and innovation.
3. **DNA/Bio-engineering Industry:** This involves advanced research into genetics and life sciences.
4. **Digital Industry:** This encompasses the ongoing shift from analog to digital technologies.



## The Importance of Smallness and Micro-Management

Huh Kyung young criticizes large corporations like Samsung for pursuing "bigness" (대기업) when they should be focusing on "smallness" (소기업) and advanced micro-technologies. He argues that true innovation lies in delving into the smallest scales, such as nanotechnology, anistrom, picometer, femtometer, and attometer. He suggests that Samsung should be investing in research that allows one to "travel" inside the human body at a microscopic level to heal organs and blood vessels. This "attometer management" (아토경영) represents the ultimate frontier of technology and a path to global dominance.



## &nbsp;The Three Types of Faces and the Need for Others

Huh Kyung young discusses three types of "faces" or perspectives:

1. **The Face One Cannot See:** This refers to one's own face, which cannot be seen directly without a mirror. This symbolizes the inability to truly see oneself without external reflection.
2. **The Face Others See:** This is how others perceive you, often through their own "mirrors" or perspectives.
3. **The Face Reflected in a Mirror:** This is a distorted or manipulated image, as mirrors can make one appear taller or more attractive than they are.

He emphasizes that because one cannot truly see their own face, it is crucial to listen to the words of others. This highlights the importance of community, colleagues, and partners in self-understanding and success. Just as a "baryon" (바리온) cannot exist alone but needs a "gluon" (글루온) to form, humans need each other.



## &nbsp;The Paradox of Paradise and Adversity

Huh Kyung young presents a paradoxical view of paradise and adversity. He states that a constantly peaceful and happy home is like a desert, while a home that experiences challenges and difficulties is like fertile ground (옥토). He argues that constant happiness leads to complacency and ultimately, a "desert-like" existence. Conversely, enduring hardships and overcoming obstacles cultivates resilience and growth, making one's life "fertile." Therefore, he encourages embracing challenges as opportunities for growth and transformation.



## &nbsp;The Mother in the Storm: A Symbol of Humanity's Savior

Huh Kyung young recounts a story about an art competition where participants were asked to draw a picture for God. The winning drawing depicted a mother holding her child amidst a raging typhoon. The child is peacefully asleep, oblivious to the storm, while the mother endures the tempest to protect her child. This image, he explains, symbolizes humanity's savior, who faces all criticism and hardship to protect the people of Earth. It represents the idea that true peace is found not in the absence of conflict, but in the ability to remain steadfast and protective amidst chaos.



## &nbsp;The Ten Principles of Health and Longevity

Huh Kyung young outlines ten principles for health and longevity, which he refers to as "smallness" (소식) and "largeness" (다식) in various aspects of life:

1. **Small Meals (소식):** Eat small, regular meals.
2. **Small Clothes (소의):** Wear simple clothing.
3. **Small Desires, Much Sleep (소욕다수):** Have few desires and sleep abundantly.
4. **Small Cars, Much Walking (소차다보):** Drive less and walk more.
5. **Small Anger, Much Laughter (소노다소):** Get angry less and laugh more.
6. **Small Talk, Much Action (소언다행):** Talk less and act more.
7. **Small Salt, Much Water (소염다수):** Consume less salt and drink more water.
8. **Small Meat, Much Vegetables (소육다채):** Eat less meat and more vegetables.
9. **Small Sugar, Much Fruit (소당다과):** Consume less sugar and more fruit.
10. **Small Greed, Much Giving (소탐다사):** Have less greed and give more.

These principles emphasize moderation, natural living, and a balanced approach to life for optimal well-being.
