

## 20140222_Lecture914_Mind_The Truth of the Mind's Core


## [English Title] The Truth of the Mind's Core: Understanding the Interplay of Mind, Consciousness, and the Spiritual Realm


## Date: February 22, 2014


## Keywords: Mind, Consciousness, Subconscious, Unconscious, Heaven and Earth


## Content


## [00:00:00] The Concepts of Yeto and Jeongto 
The concepts of Yeto and Jeongto are introduced, representing polluted and pure lands, respectively. Yeto is described as a "dirty land" where humans currently reside, a mixture of both Yeto and Jeongto. The goal is to transform this polluted land into a pure one through microorganisms and fermentation, similar to how traditional fermented foods are made. This transformation extends to our minds, as 99.9% of people are said to be in Yeto, while only 0.1% are in Jeongto. Jeongto is equated with the land of Buddha, a pure land, often referred to as "Bulguk Jeongto" (Buddha's Pure Land). In contrast, "In-gan Yeto" refers to the human realm, which is considered impure.


## [00:03:00] Addressing Societal Issues and Financial Laws 
Huh Kyung young recounts a phone call from a 70-year-old man in Haenam who had watched all his lectures. The man's farmland was at risk of being seized by the agricultural cooperative due to unpaid interest on a loan, which was based on the official land price, only one-tenth of the market value. The man requested that Huh Kyung young, if he becomes president, change the agricultural land law and loan regulations, specifically the "loss of benefit of time" law, which allows land ownership to transfer through auction if a deadline is missed. Huh Kyung young promised that if he becomes president, he would eliminate overdue interest for loans taken by common people, especially farmers, and instead reduce interest when they struggle to pay. He also pledged to allow farmers to take out loans based on the market value of their land, not just the official price, even if it means some loss for the state.


## [00:07:11] The Analogy of Eating Fruit: Perspective and Life's Journey 
Huh Kyung young presents an analogy of two people eating ten fruits. One person always chooses the best-looking fruit first, while the other consistently picks the ugliest. The person who eats the best-looking fruits always experiences the joy of consuming delicious fruit, even until the last one. Conversely, the person who eats the ugliest fruits first experiences continuous dissatisfaction, leading to a life perceived as unhappy. This illustrates that the quality of one's life is not determined by external circumstances but by one's perspective and choices. The person who always chooses the best is seen as positive, while the one who chooses the worst is negative. This highlights the importance of one's "simji" (mind's core) in shaping their experience.


## [00:11:00] The Nature of Mind, Thought, and Consciousness 
The discussion delves into the nature of the mind, thought, and consciousness. The mind is described as the "house of thought," implying that the mind itself is not inherently good or bad, but rather a vessel for thoughts. Thoughts, in turn, are the "expression of the mind" and are considered the "house of consciousness." Consciousness is further explained as the "house of the subconscious and unconscious." The unconscious is linked to past lives, while the subconscious is like something submerged underwater, driving the conscious world. Our environment and upbringing significantly influence our subconscious. For example, a child raised near a cemetery might imitate the sounds of mourning, while a child raised in a temple might be reminded of it by the sound of a wooden moktak. Therefore, understanding what is embedded in one's subconscious is crucial, as it dictates one's actions and destiny. By changing our consciousness through learning and self-awareness, our thoughts improve, leading to positive changes in our minds.


## [00:16:00] Managing Emotional Responses and the Role of the Subconscious 
Huh Kyung young illustrates how emotional responses can escalate conflicts. When a wife is furious (at 100 degrees Celsius), the husband, who was initially calm (at 0 degrees Celsius), should not respond with equal anger. Doing so would raise the emotional temperature to 200 degrees, leading to disaster. Instead, the husband should allow time for the wife's anger to cool down, as a boiling pot of water doesn't cool instantly. This emphasizes the importance of de-escalation and giving space for emotions to subside. The root of such emotional outbursts and habitual reactions is attributed to the subconscious and unconscious.


## [00:18:00] The Mind as Soil: Cultivating Goodness 
The analogy of Yeto and Jeongto is revisited, comparing the mind to soil. Yeto, the polluted land, is where seeds struggle to grow, while Jeongto, the fertile land, allows them to flourish. Similarly, if the mind is like soil, planting good seeds (thoughts) will yield good results, while planting poisonous weeds will produce poison. The mind is described as an encyclopedia and a magic box, capable of manifesting whatever is put into it. This highlights the profound impact of our thoughts and intentions on our lives.


## [00:20:00] The Relationship Between Heaven, Earth, and the Human Mind 
Huh Kyung young clarifies the relationship between Heaven, Earth, and the human mind. He asserts that the mind is not akin to Heaven but rather to Earth. Many people mistakenly associate the mind with Heaven, but the mind is like the ground, where countless thoughts and "weeds" can grow. Heaven, on the other hand, is pure and without weeds. This distinction is crucial because it implies that humans have the power to cultivate their inner "soil." All religions teach that what is resolved on Earth will be resolved in Heaven, and what is not resolved on Earth cannot be resolved in Heaven. This means that humans, as "Earth," are primary, and Heaven follows their actions. If humans sow good seeds, Heaven will bless them; if they sow bad seeds, Heaven will not intervene to fix it. Therefore, humans are not robots, and their actions on Earth dictate the response from Heaven.


## [00:23:00] The Middle Path and True Compassion 
Revisiting the fruit analogy, Huh Kyung young criticizes both the person who only eats the best fruit and the one who only eats the worst. Both extremes are seen as "insane." The true middle path involves eating what is available without preference. However, a higher level of the middle path involves considering others first. For example, if one finds a delicious fruit, their first thought should be to offer it to their parents-in-law or elders, not their own children. Prioritizing one's children over elders is considered a "sin" and plants "weeds" in the family, leading to a lack of future prospects. This emphasizes the importance of filial piety and selfless consideration for others, especially elders, as a foundation for a prosperous family.


## [00:26:00] The Principle of "Earth First" and Filial Piety 
The principle of "Earth first" is further elaborated, stating that Heaven responds to the actions of Earth. Just as a daughter-in-law's respectful actions towards her parents-in-law will lead to blessings from them, human actions on Earth precede divine intervention. It is not the parents-in-law who should initiate kindness, but the daughter-in-law who should first offer respect and care. This applies to children and parents as well; children should prioritize their parents' well-being. This "Earth first" principle is crucial for societal change, as a society that reverses this order will continue to produce "unrighteous children."


## [00:27:00] The Nature of Love: Descending vs. Ascending 
Huh Kyung young distinguishes between two types of love: "descending love" and "ascending love." Descending love is the natural love of parents for their children, especially the youngest, whom they worry about most. This love flows from top to bottom. However, true love, or "ascending love," flows from bottom to top. This means that children's love and respect for their parents, and humanity's devotion to God, are what truly activate blessings from above. He argues that children who succeed often do so not because their parents provided everything, but because they felt a sense of responsibility and love for their struggling parents. This "ascending love" motivates them to work hard and achieve.


## [00:32:00] The Malaya-vijnana and Alaya-vijnana 
The concepts of Malaya-vijnana and Alaya-vijnana (storehouse consciousness) are introduced, which are deeper levels of consciousness. The world we inhabit is influenced by the Alaya-vijnana. The "Eui-jeong-guk" (Righteous Nation) law states that the environment brightens according to the mind's thoughts. However, Huh Kyung young clarifies that it is the other way around: a bright mind leads to a bright environment. The mind's causes originate from the mind itself.


## [00:33:00] The Principle of "Bul-i" (Non-Duality) in Buddhism 
Huh Kyung young explains the Buddhist concept of "Bul-i" (non-duality), which means "not two." He emphasizes that "not two" does not mean "one." For example, a mother-in-law and daughter-in-law are not one, nor are a husband and wife. Attempting to force them into "one" leads to totalitarianism or communism. Buddhism acknowledges the distinctiveness of two entities while recognizing their interconnectedness. "Form is not different from emptiness, emptiness is not different from form" (色不異空 空不異色) illustrates this. While they are not strictly different, they are also not one. Each individual is a Buddha, but not all Buddhas are one unified entity. This principle respects the uniqueness of each being.


## [00:37:00] Religious Demographics and the Uniqueness of Huh Kyung young's Energy 
Huh Kyung young cites statistics showing that Christianity has a relatively small global following (around 200 million out of 7 billion people) and is declining, while Buddhism is viewed more favorably. He attributes this to Christianity's emphasis on a "sole God" (유일신), which he contrasts with Buddhism's "Bul-i" principle that acknowledges and embraces diversity. He claims that his own name and image possess a unique energy that strengthens people, unlike other figures or objects which cause energy to drain. He demonstrates this by having audience members test their strength while thinking of different names or looking at various pictures, showing that only thinking of or looking at Huh Kyung young's image increases their physical strength. This "zero-point energy" is described as an energy that humans do not possess.


## [00:46:00] Huh Kyung young's Healing Abilities and the Impact of Environment 
Huh Kyung young demonstrates his ability to heal physical ailments by simply looking at or touching individuals. He performs a series of tests on a young student from Gyeongsang Province, who traveled early in the morning to attend the lecture. He diagnoses the student with rhinitis, thyroid issues, and blocked brain blood vessels, attributing these to growing up in "Yeto" (polluted environment) and consuming unhealthy foods like trans fats. By looking into the student's eyes and touching their head and thyroid, he claims to instantly heal these conditions, which is verified by the student's increased strength. He emphasizes that his energy is the opposite of human energy, and simply being near him or seeing his image can bring about positive changes.


## [00:54:00] Healing Vision and the Power of Spiritual Sight 
Huh Kyung young continues his healing demonstrations by addressing the student's poor eyesight (0.01 vision). He explains that his own vision was 4.0, like that of people from Russia, Finland, and Mongolia, and that his eyes possess a spiritual energy that can heal. After looking into the student's eyes and touching them, he claims to have restored their vision, which is again confirmed by the student's increased strength. He reiterates that his healing power is not "qi" (energy) but "yeong" (spirit), which can transcend distance and physical presence.


## [00:57:00] The Ten Commandments: Earthly vs. Heavenly 
Huh Kyung young analyzes the Ten Commandments, dividing them into two categories: earthly and heavenly. The commandments against coveting, lying, stealing, adultery, and murder are considered "earthly crimes" and are fundamental laws in all societies. The commandments against dishonoring parents, keeping the Sabbath, taking God's name in vain, worshipping idols, and having no other gods before God are considered "heavenly" or related to parents. He reinterprets these "heavenly" commandments in a familial context: keeping the Sabbath means visiting parents, not taking God's name in vain means not shaming one's parents' name, and not worshipping idols means not prioritizing children or grandchildren over parents. This emphasizes that the "Earth first" principle applies to family relationships, where children's actions towards parents dictate the family's well-being.


## [01:03:00] The Eight Blessings of Jesus and the Concept of "Spiritual Poverty" 
Huh Kyung young compares the Ten Commandments of Moses with the Eight Blessings (Beatitudes) of Jesus. He notes that Jesus' teachings are of a higher dimension, focusing on the "simji" (mind's core). He condenses Jesus' teaching "Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven" into "Yeongbin Cheonguk" (Spiritual Poverty, Heavenly Kingdom). He explains that "spiritual poverty" does not mean emptying the mind, which is impossible due to the constant generation of thoughts from consciousness, subconsciousness, and unconsciousness. Instead, it means acknowledging one's insignificance before Heaven and God.


## [01:08:00] The Story of Lee Eo-ryeong and the Realization of Spiritual Poverty 
Huh Kyung young illustrates "spiritual poverty" with the story of Lee Eo-ryeong, a former Minister of Culture who initially disliked religion. Lee Eo-ryeong, a highly intellectual man, believed in self-reliance. However, when his daughter became blind due to cancer, his intellect and knowledge could not help. In desperation, he went to church with his daughter and prayed for her healing. When her eyesight miraculously returned, he realized the limits of human intellect and the existence of a higher power. Although his daughter later died from cancer, Lee Eo-ryeong kept his promise to God and became a Christian evangelist. Huh Kyung young uses this story to emphasize that true wisdom lies in acknowledging one's limitations before Heaven and not relying solely on intellect or worldly achievements.


## [01:10:00] Idols and the Importance of Non-Attachment 
Huh Kyung young defines idols as anything that takes precedence over God or parents, including power, wealth, and even children. He states that everything can become an idol if one becomes attached to it. To avoid this, one must practice "spiritual poverty" (Yeongbin Cheonguk), which is synonymous with non-attachment. Excessive attachment to diamonds, possessions, or even one's own children can turn them into idols, leading to imbalance and potential downfall. The key is to maintain a "middle path" and not prioritize these things above one's true spiritual connection.


## [01:12:00] The Meaning of "Mourning" and "Meekness" in the Eight Blessings 
Huh Kyung young continues to explain the Eight Blessings. "Blessed are those who mourn, for they will be comforted" (애통 위로) refers to mourning for one's sins against Heaven and humanity, acknowledging one's shortcomings as a "sinner." This includes feeling remorse for not being a dutiful child to one's parents. True mourning, he explains, often comes after experiencing parenthood oneself, realizing the sacrifices parents make. "Blessed are the meek, for they will inherit the earth" (온유 지읒) means that those who are gentle, compassionate, and forgiving towards others will be blessed with abundance, including wealth and land. This aligns with Buddhist principles of great compassion. Meek individuals are often patient and humble, leading to long-term prosperity, unlike those who are impulsive and quickly lose what they gain.


## [01:18:00] The Blessings of Righteousness, Mercy, and Purity of Heart 
"Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be filled" (의의 주린 자) means that those who diligently seek truth and knowledge will be provided for and will not have to worry about their livelihood. This includes success in careers and relationships. "Blessed are the merciful, for they will be shown mercy" (긍휼) signifies that those who show compassion to others will receive compassion from Heaven. This is a principle of reciprocity, where a compassionate heart leads to a compassionate response. "Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God" (심결) means that those with a clean heart can perceive God's presence and receive divine guidance, such as warnings in dreams or through unexpected events.


## [01:21:00] The Blessings of Peacemaking and Persecution for Righteousness 
"Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called children of God" (화평) means that those who live harmoniously with others and avoid conflict are considered divine. "Blessed are those who are persecuted because of righteousness, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven" (의핍 천국) is the final blessing. This implies that those who speak truth and act righteeously will face persecution, but this persecution is a path to the Kingdom of Heaven. Huh Kyung young uses himself as an example, stating that those who spread his teachings will be called "crazy" or "fraudulent," but this is the very path to heaven. He emphasizes that righteousness is like a lamb, gentle and pure, and that one must humble oneself beneath it.
