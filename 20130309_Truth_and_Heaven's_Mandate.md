## 00:00:00 The Importance of Compassion and Justice

There are many different destinies in life . When dealing with people, one should treat them with utmost respect, like a spring breeze . This means being gentle, not causing discomfort or irritation, and being polite . This concept is expressed in ancient Chinese texts as "treating others like the spring breeze" . However, when it comes to oneself, one should be as strict as an autumn frost, like a harsh winter wind . This is akin to a general on the battlefield, who must be stern and unyielding to maintain order and ensure survival . This sternness towards oneself is called Heaven's Mandate .

While one should be like a spring breeze towards parents and others, one must be as strict as autumn frost when reflecting on oneself . For example, if a mother forbids something, it must not be eaten, no matter how delicious . Huh Kyung young exemplifies this by never indulging in personal pleasures like coffee or soda, always holding himself to the highest standards . Yet, he shows immense compassion to others, even murderers and adulterers, believing that only those without sin should cast the first stone . This balance of compassion for others and strictness for oneself is the essence of truth and justice . Truth is like the spring breeze, while justice is like the autumn frost . When one sacrifices for the nation, it is an act of justice, like a soldier facing death for their country . This concept of justice can differ between nations, but the truth given by heaven is universal .



## 00:00:32 The Story of Esther and the Consequences of Karma

The story of Esther from the Bible illustrates this wisdom . When Haman, the Persian prime minister, plotted to kill all Jews, Esther's uncle informed her . Esther, with great wisdom, did not immediately confront the king but instead invited him and Haman to a private dinner . During the dinner, after serving them wine, she revealed Haman's plot to the king, leading to Haman's immediate arrest and execution . Esther's strategic approach, changing the setting and timing, allowed her to save her people . This demonstrates that confronting issues directly in an emotional setting often leads to failure, especially when dealing with powerful individuals .

This story also highlights the profound impact of individual actions, or karma . The speaker suggests that the Holocaust, where Hitler killed six million Jews, originated from a small act of disrespect by a Jewish person towards Haman, or a Jewish butcher's mistreatment of Hitler's mother . This single act of unkindness, treating a person worse than a dog, led to the extermination of an entire race . This emphasizes that our actions, even seemingly minor ones, can have devastating consequences not just for ourselves but for our entire community . The speaker also mentions how the Korean people survived 5,000 years by adopting a "spring breeze" attitude towards powerful nations like China, avoiding conflict and maintaining their independence .



## 00:02:00 The Nature of Truth: Permanent vs. Changing

Truth is categorized into two types: permanent and changing . Permanent truths include fundamental aspects of existence like parents, siblings, heaven and earth, the universe, and the seasons . These truths are immutable and do not change with time or culture . Changing truths, on the other hand, are historical and cultural, such as fashion or hairstyles . What was considered normal in one era, like a topknot in the Joseon Dynasty, might be seen as strange today . These changing truths are often driven by culture and commerce, creating industries like barber shops . While culture evolves, permanent truth remains constant . Acting upon this unchanging truth is defined as justice .



## 00:02:40 Understanding Providence: God's Unfathomable Plan

The speaker introduces the concept of "providence" (섭리), which refers to God's unfathomable plan that humans cannot comprehend . While truth is something humans can understand and practice, providence is beyond human understanding . For example, Jesus' crucifixion, despite his innocence, was an act of providence, not justice . Similarly, Huh Kyung young's ability to heal people with a glance is providence, not a medical truth . This healing is not always granted, as those with heavy karma may be refused . Providence operates on a divine level, often appearing contradictory to human logic . The story of Job, who suffered immensely despite his righteousness, is presented as an example of providence, a test of faith to bestow greater glory .



## 00:03:30 The Illusion of the Present and the Eight Sufferings

The speaker asserts that our current reality is largely an illusion . The "present" is a fleeting moment, lasting only about five seconds, before it quickly becomes the past . Even significant events, like acquiring 50 billion won, are only "ours" for a brief five seconds before they too recede into the past . This constant flow into the past means that what we perceive as reality is transient and illusory . The "eight sufferings" (팔고) in Buddhism are also described as illusions . Understanding this transient nature of reality helps us to not be overly attached to suffering or joy .



## 00:04:00 The Hierarchy of Nature and Human Systems

The speaker outlines a natural hierarchy, starting with the largest and moving to the smallest: the human mind, heaven, mountains, streams, rivers, and finally the sea . The human mind is considered the largest, capable of encompassing even the vast Pacific Ocean . Heaven is the "mother" of mountains, which in turn are the mothers of streams, leading to rivers and then the sea . This hierarchy represents an unchanging truth . Humans, however, create laws to control and apply truth in reality . These laws vary by nation, reflecting different interpretations and applications of truth . For example, some nations forbid alcohol and tobacco, while others permit them .

Laws are controlled by those in power, who are themselves controlled by heaven . Therefore, those in power must possess good fortune, which is bestowed by heaven upon those who uphold truth . Judges, who uphold the law, must possess virtue (덕) . Virtue is considered superior to wisdom (지혜) . Wisdom serves as a tool for virtue, enabling good governance . The concept of virtue was introduced by politicians during the Sui Dynasty in China to control the wise and maintain order . This historical development shows how virtue became a means to manage and lead those with wisdom .



## 00:05:00 The Virtues of the Rose of Sharon and Women

The Rose of Sharon (무궁화), Korea's national flower, embodies four virtues: cleanliness, sacrifice, permanence, and diligence . It is clean, blooms continuously, is diligent, and sacrifices itself by providing nectar for insects . Unlike roses, which are sterile and repel insects, the Rose of Sharon nourishes all living things, symbolizing the Korean people's generosity and willingness to share .

Similarly, women are said to possess four virtues: skill (솜씨), grace (맵시), speech (말씨), and heart (맘씨) . These virtues are essential for a woman to be considered valuable . Maintaining one's appearance (용모) is also a significant virtue . These virtues, like those of the Rose of Sharon, reflect a deep connection to truth and a harmonious way of living .



## 00:06:00 The Dangers of Chasing Material Wealth

The speaker warns against the dangers of chasing material wealth, comparing it to a hunter chasing a deer who cannot see the mountain . Those who pursue "a fortune in a single stroke" (일확천금) become blind to people and truth . They may dismiss even saints as "trash" and fail to recognize the true value of things until they are in dire need . This pursuit of wealth leads people to ignore truth, ethics, and morality, causing societal breakdown .

A tragic example is given of a restaurant where a grandmother, mistaking rat poison for seasoning, accidentally killed one customer and sickened five others . The speaker argues that the grandmother did not intentionally violate truth, but the deceased customer may have suffered due to their own past karma . This illustrates that sometimes, seemingly random misfortunes are a result of providence, not a direct violation of truth .



## 00:07:00 The Eight Blessings and the Eight Winds

The speaker connects the concept of truth to the "Eight Blessings" (팔복) from the Bible, which Jesus first preached . These blessings, such as "blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven," are considered truths necessary for humans . They represent a transformation from misfortune to blessing, both in life and after death .

The "Eight Winds" (팔풍) in Buddhism are also discussed, representing the eight worldly conditions that can sway people: gain and loss, praise and blame, happiness and sorrow, and truth and falsehood . To avoid the "Eight Calamities" (팔란), one must view these eight conditions as equal, understanding that what appears as loss can be gain, and what seems like misfortune can be a blessing . The speaker emphasizes that those who do not uphold truth and justice will encounter these calamities . He also shares a personal anecdote about how he is constantly afflicted with minor ailments to prevent him from being distracted from his mission of unifying the world .



## 00:08:00 The Power of Huh Kyung young's Name and Water Transformation

Huh Kyung young demonstrates a unique ability to transform water by simply calling his name . He explains that ordinary water molecules (H2O) have a hydrogen-oxygen bond angle of about 105 degrees, which makes them less absorbable by the human body and contributes to aging . However, when his name is invoked, the water's molecular structure changes, increasing the bond angle to approximately 120 degrees . This "nuclear magnetic resonance" (핵공명) makes the water highly absorbable, capable of healing all diseases, from heart conditions to the common cold .

He illustrates this by having an audience member hold his hand out. When the audience member calls Huh Kyung young's name, their hand becomes incredibly strong and difficult to pull away . Conversely, calling another person's name, even a powerful figure, results in no such effect . This transformation occurs even if his name is invoked silently in one's mind . Huh Kyung young claims this transformed water is the "world's best water" and will eventually bring him immense wealth and influence globally . He also states that he uses this water to maintain his own health, as he is divinely controlled to prevent him from healing himself directly .
