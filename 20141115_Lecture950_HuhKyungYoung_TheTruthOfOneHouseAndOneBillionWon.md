

## 20141115_Lecture950_HuhKyungYoung_TheTruthOfOneHouseAndOneBillionWon

## Keywords: Huh Kyung young, Housing Policy, Economic Policy, Welfare Policy, National Budget


## Content


## The Illusion of Government Housing Policies 
Huh Kyung young criticizes the government's approach to housing, particularly its "happy housing" initiative, which aims to supply small homes. He argues that while the government provides many houses annually, these are primarily rental units, not free homes, and only a limited number of people, such as 330,000 newlywed couples, benefit from a lottery system. He likens this to offering only the broth of a beef stew, rather than the meat itself, implying that the policy provides superficial benefits without addressing the core problem. Huh Kyung young expresses frustration with politicians who propose such policies, stating that they are misguided and detrimental to the national economy. He believes these policies are insufficient to meet the needs of the population, especially considering the large number of people getting married each year.


## Huh Kyung young's Revolutionary Proposals: Marriage and Childbirth Incentives 
Huh Kyung young outlines his own radical proposals, which he claims he has been advocating for two decades. He proposes giving 100 million won to newlywed couples, not as a loan, but as a direct grant to alleviate the financial burden of early marriage. This initiative, he explains, would be a "plus alpha" to help young people start their lives. He also suggests providing 30 million won for each child born, addressing the issue of low birth rates. These proposals, he asserts, are designed to directly benefit citizens and stimulate the economy by injecting money into the market. He contrasts his direct approach with the government's current welfare policies, which he views as inefficient and often fraudulent.


## Critique of Current Welfare and Economic Policies 
Huh Kyung young argues that existing welfare programs, such as national pensions and various subsidies, are ineffective and often exploit the public. He points out that many people struggle with rent and housing despite these programs. He criticizes the government's selective welfare, which he believes disproportionately benefits the wealthy while burdening the middle class and the poor. He specifically mentions former President Park Geun-hye's promise of 700,000 won in elderly benefits, which he claims was an attempt to mimic his own proposals but was ultimately unfeasible. Huh Kyung young advocates for abolishing the national pension system and instead distributing accumulated funds directly to all citizens, arguing that this would be a more equitable and effective use of public money.


## Comprehensive Welfare for Families and the Elderly 
Huh Kyung young extends his welfare vision to support families and the elderly. He proposes providing a full-time homemaker allowance until children enter middle school, enabling parents to raise their children without financial strain. This, he argues, would prevent children from growing up without parental care due to both parents working. He emphasizes that these benefits should be universally applied, not selectively, to ensure fairness and broad impact. He contrasts this with "selective welfare" policies, which he believes create division and dissatisfaction. His proposals aim to create a society where all citizens feel supported and valued, from newlyweds to the elderly.


## The Problem of Government Waste and Corruption 
Huh Kyung young vehemently criticizes the government's handling of the national budget, alleging widespread corruption and waste. He points out that politicians often allocate funds to their own constituencies for unnecessary projects, leading to significant financial leakage through lobbying and inflated costs. He cites examples of massive budgets allocated for "resource development" that yield no tangible results, with funds disappearing into the pockets of corrupt officials. He claims that the national budget of 370 trillion won is largely mismanaged, with a substantial portion being siphoned off by "thieves" within the government and bureaucracy. He asserts that these corrupt practices prevent the nation's wealth from truly benefiting its citizens.


## Huh Kyung young's Vision for National Dividend and Economic Reform 
To combat corruption and ensure equitable distribution of wealth, Huh Kyung young proposes a "National Dividend System." Under this system, a significant portion of the national budget would be directly distributed to citizens' bank accounts, either twice or thrice a year. He argues that this direct distribution would empower citizens and prevent the misuse of public funds by politicians and bureaucrats. He also suggests consolidating various taxes into a single, transparent system to simplify financial management and increase accountability. This radical approach, he believes, would transform the national economy, making it more transparent, efficient, and beneficial for all citizens.


## Historical Economic Context and Growth Comparison 
Huh Kyung young provides a historical overview of South Korea's economic development, comparing its growth to other Asian countries. He notes that in the early 1960s, South Korea was one of the poorest nations, with a GDP per capita significantly lower than countries like Thailand, the Philippines, and even North Korea. He highlights that North Korea's GDP was nine times higher than South Korea's at that time. However, he emphasizes that South Korea's economy has since grown exponentially, increasing by approximately 430 times, while Japan's grew by 100 times, and other Asian nations saw much smaller increases. This dramatic growth, he attributes to the foundational efforts of leaders like President Park Chung-hee.


## The Legacy of Park Chung-hee and National Pride 
Huh Kyung young praises President Park Chung-hee for his pivotal role in South Korea's economic transformation. He recounts how Park Chung-hee, despite facing criticism and being labeled a "pro-Japanese collaborator," accepted reparations from Japan to rebuild the nation's economy. Huh Kyung young contrasts this with North Korea's Kim Il-sung, who refused Japanese aid, prioritizing national pride over economic recovery. He also highlights Park Chung-hee's visit to Germany, where he was inspired by Hitler's infrastructure projects, such as the Autobahn, which were built to create jobs and stimulate the economy. Huh Kyung young argues that Park Chung-hee's pragmatic decisions, though controversial, laid the groundwork for South Korea's prosperity, and he criticizes those who condemn Park Chung-hee without understanding the historical context and the sacrifices made.


## The Importance of Traditional Values and Community Spirit 
Huh Kyung young laments the decline of traditional Korean values and community spirit, which he believes are essential for national well-being. He introduces the concept of "Bang-si" (방시), a traditional practice of offering hospitality and food to travelers, emphasizing its role in fostering generosity and community bonds. He suggests that reviving such practices, along with "Pum-a-si" (품앗이), a traditional system of communal labor exchange, would strengthen social cohesion and alleviate many of the problems faced by modern society. He criticizes the current individualistic society, where people are isolated and lack support, leading to increased rates of suicide and social distress. He proposes creating communal housing and reviving traditional community centers to foster a sense of belonging and mutual support.


## The Wisdom of Ancestors: Health and Longevity 
Huh Kyung young delves into traditional wisdom regarding health and longevity, particularly focusing on the importance of hydration for the elderly. He explains that ancestors understood the critical need for older individuals to drink water regularly, especially during the night, to prevent strokes and other health issues. He criticizes modern society for neglecting such simple yet vital practices, leading to increased health problems among the elderly. He recounts traditional customs where daughters-in-law would bring water to their parents-in-law's bedside, highlighting the deep respect and care embedded in these practices. He emphasizes that proper hydration, especially for those over 50, is crucial for maintaining blood circulation and preventing serious illnesses like stroke, which he attributes to insufficient water intake.


## The Power of Huh Kyung young's Message and Future Impact 
Huh Kyung young concludes by reiterating the profound impact of his teachings and proposals. He states that his lectures, though initially dismissed as "comedy" by some, contain deep truths that will eventually be recognized. He believes that his ideas, such as the National Dividend and comprehensive welfare programs, are not only feasible but necessary for the nation's survival and prosperity. He asserts that his message, once widely disseminated, will awaken the public and force politicians to adopt more effective and equitable policies. He envisions a future where his principles guide the nation, leading to a society where all citizens are healthy, prosperous, and live in harmony.
