## The Truth of Coincidence, Rationality, Providence, and Creation 
Today's lecture delves into the truth of coincidence, rationality, providence, and creation, which can also be termed the truth of necessity and creation. This concept can be understood as the theory of natural spontaneity (무의 자연설), suggesting that all things in the universe came into being by chance. This aligns with the philosophies of figures like Laozi and Confucianism. Conversely, the concept of necessity and creation posits that there is a creator, that the Earth was made out of necessity, and that everything was scientifically created. This perspective is often associated with Christianity. The term "Christianity" (기독교) itself, as explained by Huh Kyung young, means "the religion that examines the root of humanity" (인간의 뿌리가 살필 독자). It explores the philosophical question of where the root of human life originates.


## Contrasting Philosophical Views: Spontaneity vs. Creation 
The theory of natural spontaneity, influenced by Confucianism and Laozi, along with the Buddhist concepts of emptiness (공) and the Middle Way (중도), suggests that the universe was formed by chance and that human existence is a result of accidental evolution, as proposed by Darwin's theory of evolution. However, Huh Kyung young argues against this, asserting that such an interpretation is problematic. He proposes a comparison between these views and the Christian perspective, which emphasizes creation. He recounts a personal anecdote of meeting someone three years prior, and then unexpectedly encountering them again at the building of Chairman Park Kyung-jae. Huh Kyung young questions whether this repeated encounter is a mere coincidence or a matter of <mark>necessity</mark> (필연).


## Necessity and Providence: Beyond Rationality 
Huh Kyung young emphasizes that such encounters should not be viewed as mere coincidences. He asserts that the concept of necessity is intertwined with providence, which he notes is translated as "providence" in English, distinct from "rationalism." He then poses a question about whether rationality and providence are opposing concepts. Rationality, in a modern scientific context, requires tangible, material evidence. Providence, however, encompasses phenomena that cannot be scientifically proven, such as the attractive forces between quanta and electrons. These are considered to fall under the domain of providence.


## The Principle of Plus and Minus Energy in Electricity 
Huh Kyung young explains the concept of electricity to illustrate a fundamental principle. Electricity involves positive and negative charges. When asked to define electricity, common answers like "natural law" or "energy transfer" are deemed incorrect. He emphasizes the importance of asking "What do you think?" to encourage critical thinking, rather than simply giving commands. This approach fosters independent decision-making.

He then elaborates on electricity: it is the energy generated when negative electrons move towards positive electrons. In power generation, a greater number of negative electrons are created, while positive electrons are kept fewer. The negative electrons strive to move towards the positive ones, and this movement generates electricity, which is energy. For example, rubbing a plastic ruler on hair makes the ruler positively charged, attracting the negatively charged hair. This demonstrates the generation of electricity.


## The Nature of Existence: Coincidence vs. Necessity 
Huh Kyung young reiterates the question of whether events, such as his meeting with the individual, are coincidental or necessary. He firmly states that <mark>coincidence does not exist</mark> in this world. He introduces the concept of "pre-established harmony" (예정 조화설), which posits that all paths humans take are predetermined. Although humans undergo various processes, they will eventually align with the will of a single Creator. Therefore, there is no need for despair, as humanity is destined to return to this providence. To use the term "coincidence" is to be an atheist. He also criticizes "rationality" as something that can harm the world.


## Providence and Human Action: The Eyes of Heaven 
Huh Kyung young illustrates the difference between rationality and providence with an example: if ten soldiers want to drink alcohol, but one refuses, that individual might be ostracized for not being "rational" in the group's context. However, that person is acting based on providence, believing that their every action is being observed by "the eyes of Heaven" through a hidden camera, which is humanity itself. He explains that the eyes of the people are the eyes of Heaven. If someone in power acts improperly, it will inevitably be exposed by the public, leading to the downfall of their authority. This suggests that there is a creator behind all events, and nothing is coincidental; everything is necessary. If people harbor ill intentions, Heaven intervenes, leading to inevitable consequences. Every human is merely a tool, and their actions are recorded by the "eyes of God," which will eventually be revealed.


## Creation and Evolution: A Unified Perspective 
Huh Kyung young asserts that evolution exists within creation. He criticizes Darwin's theory of evolution as incomplete, stating that creation encompasses evolution. He believes that if one lives with a good heart and acts righteously, believing in creation, positive outcomes will follow. Conversely, engaging in negative behaviors will lead to negative consequences.


## The Inevitability of Consequences: Beyond Coincidence 
Huh Kyung young addresses the argument that a murderer might claim their actions were "necessary" rather than coincidental. He explains that the path leading to someone becoming a murderer is a necessary outcome of all the actions of their ancestors and themselves. If that person had listened to prophets, pastors, or priests who taught not to harm others, they would not have reached that point. The mistake humans make is in confusing the result with coincidence. There is no such thing as a "coincidental" car accident or bad luck; everything is necessary.


## The Principle of Plus and Minus in Relationships and Communication 
Huh Kyung young extends the concept of plus and minus energy to human relationships, particularly within families. He likens men to "plus" and women to "minus." A household with more "minus" energy (women) tends to thrive, as the negative current flows towards the positive. He cites a U.S. psychological study showing an 85% difference in divorce rates between families with only sons and those with only daughters, suggesting that families with more daughters have lower divorce rates.

He advises that if a couple has a son first, the husband should act more like a "daughter" towards his wife, providing energy and speaking gently. He then analyzes the word "speech" (S-P-E-E-C-H) in terms of plus and minus. The "plus" elements are S (sincerity), P (passion), and E (emotion). Excessive use of these in marital conversations can lead to arguments. The "minus" elements are E (easy), C (cantabile/rhythmic), and H (humor). He suggests that communication should be easy, rhythmic, and humorous, with less sincerity, passion, and emotion. This is why salespeople often use 90% "minus" communication and only 10% product explanation. Similarly, sermons by pastors should incorporate more "minus" elements like humor and storytelling to foster church growth.


## The Importance of "Minus" Energy and Humility 
Huh Kyung young emphasizes that "minus" energy should be abundant. He explains that "minus" represents humility, while "plus" represents elevation, like Heaven. Just as all water flows to the lowest point (the sea), people are drawn to those who are humble. Therefore, simply presenting logical arguments will not be effective; one must create "minus" current. He concludes that understanding providence and necessity is crucial to understanding creation.


## The Creator's Design and the Speed of Electrons

He claims to possess the wisdom to manipulate these electrons, suggesting that a machine generating electrons could power an airplane to reach other galaxies at speeds far exceeding light, in the blink of an eye. This energy, derived from the movement of negative electrons towards positive ones, will be harnessed by humans when they understand his true identity.


## The Creator's Simple Yet Infinite Design: Atomic Elements 
Huh Kyung young explains that God created an infinite array of substances and life forms using just eight fundamental elements out of 118. He illustrates this with atomic numbers: hydrogen (1), helium (2), lithium (3), beryllium (4), boron (5), carbon (6), nitrogen (7), and oxygen (8). He explains how slight variations in the number of hydrogen atoms can transform one element into another. For instance, adding two hydrogen atoms to carbon creates oxygen, while removing one hydrogen atom from oxygen creates nitrogen, a vital nutrient for plants. This demonstrates the Creator's simple yet powerful design, where a few basic elements can be combined to create an infinite number of substances. He also mentions that alchemists attempted to create gold from lead by manipulating hydrogen atoms, as gold has 79 hydrogen atoms and silver has 78. This principle of combination and transformation is the essence of creation, which is predetermined.


## The Flaws of the Theory of Causality and the Importance of "Minus" Energy in Family 
Huh Kyung young criticizes the Buddhist theory of causality (인연설), which states that "this exists because that exists, and that exists because this exists." He argues that this theory fails to explain the ultimate origin of "this." He asserts that the existence of a creator who made the eight fundamental elements is what allows for the infinite variety of matter.

He returns to the topic of family, reiterating that having a first daughter reduces the divorce rate by 80%, according to U.S. research. This is because a daughter increases the "minus" (feminine) energy in the household, which attracts the "plus" (masculine) energy. He states that a man is blessed if he has three women in his life (mother, wife, daughter-in-law), and even more so with a daughter. He advises men to use "minus" language (gentle, humorous, rhythmic) with their wives to create a positive atmosphere, rather than always being "plus" (direct, demanding), which can lead to conflict. God is always in the low places, and providence is like a "minus" current. Therefore, one must be cautious of "plus" currents.


## The Hidden Meanings in Language and the Power of "Minus" Responses 
Huh Kyung young claims to be the first in Korea to explain the hidden meanings in language, referencing the Ramada Sutra, Chapter 38, which he connects to the biblical story of Jesus feeding the five thousand with two fish and five loaves of bread (오병이어). He also explains that his own name, "Yesu" (예수), means "the head of prophecy," indicating his role in foretelling the future.

He then distinguishes between "inyeon" (인연, karmic connection) and "piryyeon" (필연, necessity). While "inyeon" refers to a process and result, it falls under coincidence. "Piryyeon" (necessity) is a predetermined path. He advises against responding to "plus" (aggressive or challenging) questions with "plus" answers, as this leads to failure. Instead, one should respond with "minus" (humorous, humble, or agreeable) answers. For example, if asked about his controversial claims of levitation, he would respond with humor and humility, acknowledging the public's need for joy in politics. He asserts that those who act with negativity are destined for negative outcomes, and those who are destined for salvation are predetermined to receive it. He clarifies that this is not about karmic connections but about God's predetermination, which transcends Buddhist and Christian interpretations.

## The Universal Order of the Cosmos and the Creator's Design 
Huh Kyung young states that all religions, despite their differences, ultimately converge on similar principles. He emphasizes that the universe is created and governed by God, and that the specific denominations or sects are secondary. He then delves into the precise order of the cosmos, explaining the different frequency cycles of sound and communication. He notes that God created sound in the beginning, and that different frequency ranges are suitable for different beings: 1-10 cycles for insects, 10-10,000 cycles for humans (audible sound), 10,000-100,000 cycles for low-frequency waves (used in broadcasting and radar), 100,000-3 million cycles for medium waves (radio), 3 million-30 million cycles for short waves (television), and 30 million-300 million cycles for ultra-short waves (wireless communication, cell phones).

He explains that the human ear is designed to hear only a specific range of frequencies, preventing us from being overwhelmed by the sounds of insects or the Earth's rotation. The Earth rotates at an astonishing speed of 106,070 km/h, yet we hear no sound. If we could, our eardrums would burst. This precise, unchanging speed of the Earth's rotation, with an error of less than one second per year, is evidence against the idea of a universe created by chance or Darwinian evolution. Those who believe in such theories are, in his view, detrimental to human culture.
