## Content


## The Distinction Between Truth and Rationality 
Huh Kyung young emphasizes that in every aspect of life, including the concept of "growing pains" and the nature of one's mind and heart, there exists a fundamental distinction between truth and its opposite. He asserts that his teachings exclusively convey truth. The antithesis of truth, he explains, is rationality. While legal judgments and political decisions, such as the dismissal of a prime minister, are often based on rationality, the public frequently misunderstands truth, being swayed by media headlines rather than comprehensive understanding. This reliance on rationality, rather than truth, aligns with what he metaphorically describes as being on the side of "ghosts" in a religious context. Truth is synonymous with providence, and its opposite is rationality. He defines truth as genuine justice, contrasting it with rationality, which he labels as injustice or unrighteousness. He contends that the current world is dominated by unrighteousness, with political powers often disguising their actions as justice, when in reality, they are driven by rational and materialistic concerns, rather than spiritual or truthful ones.


## The Peril of Misguided Language and "Great" Concepts 
Huh Kyung young illustrates the dangers of using certain words and concepts, particularly those involving the character "대" (dae), meaning "great" or "big," which he associates with misfortune. He recounts how he predicted negative outcomes based on such linguistic choices. For instance, when the phrase "unification is a jackpot" (통일은 대박이다) was used by a president, he immediately foresaw significant national suffering. He interprets "대박" (daebak) as "big boat," and a big boat "unifying" with water signifies sinking and immense loss of life. He argues that a boat must remain separate from water to survive, just as opposing forces are necessary for life. Therefore, using such language for national matters can lead to disasters like shipwrecks, gun violence, and national upheaval. He suggests that "unification is our suffering" or "unification is our heaviest burden" would be more appropriate and grounding for the nation.

He extends this linguistic analysis to corporate and personal names. He notes that companies with "대" (dae) in their names, such as "대한전선" (Daehan Electric Wire) and "대우" (Daewoo), have often faced catastrophic collapses after initial success. Similarly, he predicted the downfall of a prime ministerial candidate named "안대" (Andae) due to the "대" character, foreseeing a short tenure and presidential embarrassment. He interprets "대" (dae) in this context as "greatly burning," signifying destruction. He cites the "대왕코너" (Daewang Corner) fire as another example of a "great" entity meeting a fiery end.


## Historical Misinterpretations and Their Consequences 
Huh Kyung young further elaborates on historical incidents where linguistic errors led to severe consequences. He recalls how the "대한일보" (Daehan Ilbo) newspaper ceased to exist on the day of Park Chung-hee's military revolution. He also mentions an incident involving the "동아일보" (Dong-A Ilbo) during Syngman Rhee's presidency, where a typographical error in the character for "president" (대통령) resulted in it being printed as "견통령" (gyeontongnyeong), meaning "dog president." This led to the newspaper's temporary suspension. He emphasizes that such seemingly minor errors, especially with the "대" character, can have profound impacts.

He then details the "대한일보" (Daehan Ilbo) incident during the 5.16 military revolution. The newspaper's headline declared "Anti-communism as the national policy" (방공을 국시로 한다). However, due to a subtle dot in the Chinese character for "공" (gong), it was misread as "우공" (ugong), implying "friend of communism." This grave error led to the permanent closure of the "대한일보," as it was perceived as endorsing communism by the new military regime. Huh Kyung young asserts that these incidents demonstrate how a single character or phrase, if misused or misinterpreted, can lead to national crises and personal ruin. He claims to possess the ability to foresee such outcomes instantly, based on his understanding of these underlying truths.


## Buddhist Philosophy and the Concept of "Maha" 
Huh Kyung young delves into Buddhist philosophy to explain the concept of "Maha" (마하), which means "great" or "immense." He recounts a conversation with an associate who questioned if Samsung's "Maha Management" (마하 경영) was related to Buddhist teachings like "Mahaprajnaparamita" (마하반야바라밀다). He explains that Buddhist teachings are tailored to the "근기" (geungi), or spiritual capacity, of individuals. He outlines a hierarchy of Buddhist scriptures:

- **First Dimension (1D):** <em>Vimalakirti Sutra</em> (유마경) – "Straight-line Buddhism," for those with lower spiritual capacity.
- **Second Dimension (2D):** <em>Mahavaipulya Sutra</em> (대방광불화엄경) – "Flat-plane Buddhism," for those with slightly higher understanding.
- **Third Dimension (3D):** <em>Prajnaparamita Sutra</em> (반야경) – "Space Buddhism," for those with higher spiritual capacity. This is where "Maha Prajnaparamita" originates.
- **Fourth Dimension (4D):** <em>Lotus Sutra</em> (법경) – "Three-dimensional Buddhism," or "Cosmic Buddhism," for the most advanced.
He states that while these scriptures are considered profound by Buddhist practitioners, he views them as elementary, akin to textbooks for primary school students. He then introduces the <em>Avatamsaka Sutra</em> (화엄경), which he considers the highest level, representing the "Avatamsaka Dharma Realm" (화엄 법계) that encompasses the entire universe. He equates "Avatamsaka" with the "quantum world" in English, where particles like molecules, atoms, neutrons, protons, and electrons move in an orderly fashion, unlike quanta (photons), which lack inherent order and move according to one's will.


## The Quantum Nature of Karma and Instantaneous Judgment 
Huh Kyung young explains that photons, being light, move according to one's mind. If one harbors negative thoughts, these photons disrupt the body's cellular order, leading to illness through the release of abnormal hormones. This concept forms the basis of the "Avatamsaka Dharma Realm," which he describes as being like the quantum world. He asserts that judgment is not a future event but occurs instantaneously, every second. Negative actions immediately trigger the release of harmful hormones, causing physical deterioration and suffering, even before any external punishment. Conversely, good deeds lead to positive physical and mental states. This "quantum" or "photon" mechanism, he explains, is responsible for the immediate manifestation of karma, a concept often misunderstood by those who only perceive order and definition.


## The "Maha Management" Fallacy and "Atto Management" 
Huh Kyung young reiterates his prediction regarding Samsung's "Maha Management." He interpreted "Maha" as "immensely great," signifying the end of Samsung's current trajectory. He predicted that Lee Kun-hee, the chairman, would suffer a heart attack, leading to his son taking over. He advised that the son should shift from the "prudent" (청색, blue) management style of his father to a "red" (빨간색) or "leading" (주도형) approach, changing the Samsung logo to reflect this. He claims that his prediction of Lee Kun-hee's heart attack within a week came true, emphasizing his ability to foresee such events.

He advocates for "Atto Management" (아토 경영) instead of "Maha Management." "Atto" refers to something extremely small, contrasting with "Maha," which signifies immense size. He argues that Samsung, represented by three stars, should focus on small-scale, future-oriented businesses like DNA-related industries, which are part of the four major industrial revolutions he foresees. These include spiritual industries, digital design, and divine nature. He stresses that focusing on "great" or "large" concepts like "Maha" will inevitably lead to problems for Samsung, whereas embracing the "atto" or quantum realm, which deals with the unseen, is the path to future success.


## The Mind (마음) and Heart (마음씨): 99.9% vs. 0.1% 
Huh Kyung young explains that the "mind" (마음) constitutes 99.9% of our being, while the "heart" or "mindset" (마음씨) accounts for only 0.1%. He likens the mind to a vast field of rice, where only a small percentage of grains are suitable for planting as seeds. He introduces Buddhist concepts of consciousness:

- **Eighth Consciousness (아뢰야식, Alaya-vijnana):** The fundamental consciousness, representing 95% of the unconscious, which is our past life's accumulated karma.
- **Seventh Consciousness (말라야식, Manas-vijnana):** The subconscious, representing 3% of our current life's accumulated experiences.
- **Sixth Consciousness (의식, Vijnana):** The conscious mind, representing 2%.
He claims that ordinary people only utilize 1% of their conscious mind, corresponding to an IQ of around 150. Using 2% would be an IQ of 230, and 100% would be an IQ of 430, which he claims to possess. He states that 99% of people are unconsciously driven by their past and present accumulations, leading them to excel in specific fields (e.g., a cleaner cleaning, a good student studying, an artist painting). He argues that forcing someone to change their innate predisposition, such as an artist becoming a surgeon, will lead to failure and even harm, as their "mind" is not aligned with that path.

He emphasizes that while the "mind" (마음) is largely unchangeable, the "heart" or "mindset" (마음씨), though only 0.1%, can be cultivated and changed. This "mindset" is the "seed" that, when planted, yields abundant "karma" (업). He explains that the "mind" is akin to past life's DNA, while the "mindset" is the current life's genes, which are quantum in nature. By changing this 0.1% "mindset," one can alter 99.9% of their reality. He states that humans can only change 1% of their destiny in one lifetime, meaning it would take 100 lifetimes to change 100%. However, scientific advancements, like modern medicine saving lives that would have been lost in the past, demonstrate how even a 1% change can have profound effects. He concludes by urging people to diligently study and live, as they can change their "mindset" even if their fundamental "mind" remains fixed.
