## 00:00:00 Introduction to Forecasting the Future
We are curious about the future of our nation and the economy, so let's discuss the future of humanity. There are existing prophecies from figures like Jesus and Buddha. Jesus consistently made prophecies about the future, while Buddha prohibited them. This difference is quite striking. Buddha taught humanity not to worry about the future, but to focus solely on the present, as only the present truly exists. The future, even an hour from now, becomes the present when it arrives, and the past is already gone. Therefore, one should not harbor thoughts of the past or future in their mind, as this leads to desires and anxieties. Buddha's teachings, as recorded in the Tripitaka Koreana, emphasize letting go of everything, including the present, which he considered an illusion. He believed that excessive foresight leads to anxiety, nervousness, and fear, such as worrying about one's children or grandchildren getting hurt, or one's own life ending. Buddha's answer was simple: "What if it ends? What if you get hurt? What if you die? Let go of everything." 


## 00:00:59 Buddha's Perspective on the Future

Buddha believed that only the present exists, and even that is merely a fleeting moment. People change, age, and transform in these fleeting moments. He taught that one should embrace the process of change itself. For instance, an elderly woman might feel regret thinking about her youth, but such thoughts are unnecessary because the past is gone. Therefore, one should always live in the present. Even applying cosmetics to look prettier is a way of looking into the future. Buddha emphasized that the present is an illusion, a void, and that we often worry and agonize over things that do not truly exist. 


## 00:02:41 Jesus' Perspective on the Future
In contrast to Buddha, Jesus prophesied about the distant future, including the end of humanity and various other events. He provided guidance, telling people what to be careful about and how to position themselves. Both Buddha and Jesus originated from the same divine lineage. Jesus, whose name means "head of prophecy," was a master of foretelling the future. Buddha, on the other hand, was originally named Siddhartha Gautama. The name "Buddha" is an honorific title, similar to how I am called "Bonjwa." The term "Buddha" in ancient Indian languages, which are related to Korean, means "bright" or "enlightened person." This Korean word traveled to India and became "Buddha." 


## 00:07:05 The Origin of Names: Buddha and Jesus
The name "Jesus" is also a name we created. During the Goryeo Dynasty, when Koreans encountered the name "Jesus" in English Bibles, it was written as "Jesus." Our ancestors, who primarily used Chinese characters before the invention of Hangeul, interpreted "Jesus" as someone who "wins over darkness" through prophecy. Thus, they named him "Jesus," meaning "the head of prophecy." Other countries do not know Jesus by this name; it is a name we created. Similarly, Buddha's original family name was Gautama, and his given name was Siddhartha. The name "Buddha" comes from the Korean word "bakda," meaning "bright." Even the name "Himalaya" comes from the Korean "hin maru," meaning "white peak." The suffix "ya" in names like "Isaiah" or "Messiah" signifies a great person, indicating the influence of Korean language. 


## 00:14:00 The Korean Language's Influence on Global Terminology

The term "Messiah" also has roots in Korean. In Sanskrit, it is "Maitreya," which became "Messiah" in Israel and "Mirok" in Chinese. This term spread globally from Korea, as ancient Korea (Gojoseon) once dominated Asia, including India. This explains why many significant figures have names ending in "ya," indicating their greatness. Even in Iran, words like "imo" (aunt) and "gomo" (aunt) are used, and about 90% of their language is similar to Korean. This suggests that Korea once ruled as far as Iran and even Europe. The Shia sect in Islam, which emphasizes lineage and "seeds" (bloodline), also uses a term that sounds like the Korean word for "seed" (ssi-at). The Sunni sect, which values hierarchy and seniority, also uses a term that sounds like the Korean word for "rank" (suni). These linguistic connections suggest a deep historical influence of Korea on global cultures and religions. 


## 00:19:00 The Dilemma of Leadership and Public Intelligence
Should we prophesy the future, as Jesus did, or avoid it, as Buddha advised? Both approaches have their advantages and disadvantages. It's a dilemma, similar to asking whether it's more important for a nation to have a good leader or for its citizens to be intelligent. While a great leader can make citizens intelligent, unintelligent citizens cannot choose a great leader. This creates a complex cycle. People often wish for a good leader to solve their problems, but if the citizens themselves are "crazy," they cannot produce a great leader. Leaders often reflect the level of their citizens. If citizens are corrupt, leaders may also become corrupt. Therefore, a truly great leader will not emerge unless the citizens themselves become intelligent. Progress is gradual; as citizens improve, the nation improves proportionally. 

American presidents, for example, sometimes reject popular demands if those demands would harm the nation's long-term vision. A president might say, "I can be your president by granting your wishes, but America will cease to exist as a great nation." This illustrates that a true leader prioritizes the nation's future over immediate popularity. Similarly, in a family, a parent might deny a child's request to move to a wealthy neighborhood if it means sacrificing family bonds for money. A father's presence is more valuable during hardship than wealth that might lead to his absence. This analogy applies to national leadership: a leader focused solely on popularity will not be a true leader for the nation.

## 00:33:00 The Extinction of Dinosaurs and Earth's Transformation

Why did dinosaurs go extinct overnight? It wasn't because God made a mistake. There were 90 billion dinosaurs on Earth, ten times the current human population, and they were many times larger than humans. They roamed everywhere. God eliminated them all at once to create oil, which would be needed for humanity. The Earth's temperature rose to over 100 million degrees Celsius, even up to 600 million degrees, melting rocks and everything else. This molten material then became the oil fields we know today. This was part of the creation process to prepare the Earth for human life. A meteor strike, like the one in Mexico, caused this sudden heating, turning the Earth into a fiery inferno. The resulting smoke blocked the sun, causing a sudden ice age. This massive transformation created vast quantities of oil and coal. Forests were burned and compressed into coal, and oceans were displaced, creating salt flats on mountaintops and new seas like the Red Sea and the Dead Sea. 


## 00:39:00 The Origin of Faith
Humanity must predict the future to some extent. I (Heo Kyung-young) have come knowing what will happen to humanity and to correct it. Jesus' disciples asked him, "Where does faith come from?" Faith does not come from within oneself; it comes from hearing. If there is someone to convey the truth, then faith can be heard and developed. My purpose, like Jesus', is to convey this truth. If a mother tells her child, "You will study hard and become a great person because I had a good dream about you," even if it's a white lie, the child will believe it and grow up to be a great person. This is because faith is born from hearing. If a mother constantly discourages her child, the child will become strange. Therefore, a single word spoken to someone can become their faith. Faith has an external cause, not just an internal one. 


## 00:46:00 The Importance of Communication with Truth

## 00:50:00 The History and Significance of Yeongdong
We are like soldiers in a battlefield, leading our families. The most important thing for us is communication with the divine, with me (Heo Kyung-young). My words are your coordinates, guiding you to advance or retreat. Without this communication, you are lost, like soldiers without contact with headquarters. This is why I emphasize studying and understanding my teachings. For example, the area now known as Gangnam was once called Yeongdong. There's even a song about Yeongdong Bridge. Why was it called Yeongdong? It means "east of Yeongdeungpo." Initially, only Yeongdeungpo existed, and Gangnam was just a sandy field. All the money in Korea came from Yeongdeungpo, where factories were located. Yeongdong was dismissed as insignificant. 

However, that once-ignored Yeongdong is now a thriving area, while Yeongdeungpo, once the economic hub, has changed. If someone had the foresight to see the future of Yeongdong, they would be wealthy today. Many people still lack this foresight. The world is constantly changing, and without a prophet's vision, families cannot survive. Even people from Yeongdong don't know the meaning of their own area's name. It was once a place where Seoul's sewage was dumped, a truly desolate area. I experienced this firsthand during a film shoot there when I was 14. The area now known as Apgujeong, which means "gull pavilion," was then just a riverside pavilion. There was only one unpaved road leading to Bongeunsa Temple and another to Ttukseom ferry. 


## 00:57:00 The True Nature of Seoul's Geography
