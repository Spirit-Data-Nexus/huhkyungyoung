## The Etymology and Significance of "Jogangjicheo" 
The term "Jogangjicheo" (糟糠之妻), referring to a wife who has shared hardships, is often associated with the surname "Jo" (趙). This is because the character "Jo" (糟) in "Jogangjicheo" signifies "dregs" or "lees," implying a wife who endured poverty and hardship, eating leftover dregs and husks. This contrasts with other surnames like "Park" or "Kim," which do not carry the same historical or linguistic connotation of steadfastness through adversity. The historical context of Chinese characters reveals that the "Jo" character was intentionally chosen to represent a wife who remains loyal and supportive even in difficult times. Such a woman, who consumes the "dregs" and "husks" of life, is considered to possess a tranquil and resilient spirit.


## The Profound Meaning of Names: Huh Kyung young's Parents 
The names of Huh Kyung young's parents hold a profound, almost fated significance when combined. His mother's name, "Gye-su" (桂樹), refers to a cassia tree, while his father's name, "Heo-nam" (許南), means "to permit the south." When these names are combined, they signify "permitting the planting of cassia trees in the southern nation." This symbolic interpretation suggests a destiny related to nurturing and establishing something significant within the nation. This interpretation also connects to the idea of a "power cassia tree," implying a spiritual or influential presence rather than a mere religious organization.


## The Symbolism of the Ox and Horse in "Jogangjicheo" 
The concept of "Jogangjicheo" can also be understood through the symbolism of the ox and the horse, representing sacrifice and endurance. The character for "ox" (牛) in "Jogangjicheo" (糟糠之妻) signifies sacrifice, much like the ox that is sacrificed. This refers to a person who lives a life of self-sacrifice. The character for "horse" (馬) in "Jogangjicheo" (糟糠之妻) represents a camel, which is a creature of immense endurance. Camels can survive for a week without food in the desert due to their ability to store fat. They also possess unique physiological adaptations, such as nostrils that can close to block sandstorms and multiple rows of eyelashes to protect their eyes. These characteristics symbolize the resilience and protective nature of a "Jogangjicheo."


## Creation and Evolution: A Unified Perspective 
The discussion extends to the relationship between creation and evolution, particularly in the context of the camel's adaptations. The existence of one-humped and two-humped camels, depending on their desert environment, is presented not as a contradiction to creation but as an example of evolution within creation. Huh Kyung young argues that God created the camel, and its adaptations to different environments are a form of evolution within that divine creation. This perspective challenges the conventional rivalry between creationism and evolution, suggesting that evolution is an inherent part of God's creative design. This historical observation led to the association of the Jo surname with enduring loyalty, particularly in the context of marriage.
