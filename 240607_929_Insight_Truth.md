## The Truth of Insight


## Date: June 7, 2014


## Keywords: Insight, National Crisis, Political Corruption, Economic Inequality, Spiritual Awakening


## Content


## The Nature of Life and National Crises
Life is not merely about indulgence, but a continuous journey of self-improvement and spiritual awakening . Huh Kyung young emphasizes that our existence on this earth is a brief period for spiritual growth and self-realization . He reflects on a tragic incident that occurred 50 days prior, expressing deep shame and a desire to renounce his nationality due to the government's mishandling of the disaster . This event, where many students perished due to incorrect decisions, brought immense sorrow, grief, and anger to the nation .

Huh Kyung young recalls the 1998 financial crisis when South Korea was on the brink of collapse, yet the nation persevered and developed . He asserts that the current crisis, involving the loss of hundreds of lives, is a result of absolute negligence and calls for accountability from those responsible for creating such a catastrophic situation . He criticizes the tendency to exploit such tragedies for political gain, highlighting a history of accumulated corruption across successive governments . He urges the nation to find hope amidst despair and move towards a new future .


## Historical Perspective and Political Dysfunction
Huh Kyung young critiques the historical trajectory of Korean politics, characterized by factionalism and infighting between political parties . He argues that despite the constant struggle and exploitation of the populace through taxation, the fundamental issues remain unresolved . He envisions a future where Korea transcends these internal conflicts to lead the world .

He then discusses prophecies regarding Korea's future, particularly the emergence of a "Heavenly Emperor" who will unify the world . He identifies a specific location on Ganghwa Island as a propitious site for this future, a place where a grand palace will be built . This location, he claims, is a "king's mountain" and a "dragon's lair," destined to become a global center . He describes how this future palace, often shrouded in mist, will appear as a celestial abode, attracting worldwide attention .


## The Importance of Insight (Tongchal)
Huh Kyung young stresses the critical importance of "insight" (통찰, <em>tongchal</em>) in understanding events and making sound judgments . He criticizes the public's tendency to be swayed by superficial narratives and political propaganda, especially during national holidays like Memorial Day . He recounts the historical significance of Memorial Day, detailing the sacrifices of American and UN forces during the Korean War, contrasting it with the government's failures and deceit . He emphasizes that true insight allows one to see beyond immediate appearances and understand the deeper causes and implications of events .

He further elaborates on the concept of insight by discussing different forms of poverty: absolute poverty, relative poverty, and "poverty in abundance" (풍요 속 빈곤, <em>pungyo sok bingon</em>) . He argues that despite material wealth, many people experience a spiritual or emotional poverty due to a lack of insight . He also introduces the concept of "ancestral poverty" (조상 대대로 물려받은 빈곤, <em>josang daedaero mullyeobadeun bingon</em>), referring to inherited limitations or mindsets . He asserts that cultivating insight can eliminate these forms of poverty and lead to a more fulfilling life .


## Economic Policies and National Governance
Huh Kyung young proposes radical economic policies aimed at national prosperity and citizen welfare. He suggests that a significant portion of the national budget, currently mismanaged or wasted, could be directly distributed to citizens as a "national dividend" . He highlights the immense savings that could be achieved by reducing political expenses, such as local government budgets, parliamentary salaries, and election costs . He argues that these savings could fund substantial welfare programs, including monthly payments to the elderly .

He advocates for a system where citizens, as "shareholders" of the nation, receive dividends from state-owned enterprises and national assets . He criticizes the current political system where politicians, acting as "managers," misappropriate these funds without accountability . He also proposes a "tax point system" where high taxpayers receive special recognition and benefits, including inheritable "national merit certificates" and guaranteed monthly stipends . This system would also grant them burial rights in national cemeteries and preferential treatment in housing development .


## Combating Corruption and Political Reform
Huh Kyung young strongly condemns political corruption, particularly bribery, and proposes a "double penalty system" (쌍벌제, <em>ssangbeolje</em>) where both the giver and receiver of bribes are punished . He suggests that whistleblowers who report bribery should receive double the amount of the bribe as a reward . He argues that such measures would deter corruption and promote transparency .

He criticizes the current political structure, including the indirect election of local government officials, which he believes leads to corruption and wasteful spending . He advocates for a system where the president directly appoints provincial governors and mayors, similar to the US electoral college system, to ensure more efficient governance and reduce political infighting . He believes that a strong, centralized leadership, like that of a "Huh Kyung young president," is necessary to implement these reforms and transform the nation .


## The Philosophy of "Trickle-Up" Economics
Huh Kyung young introduces the concept of "trickle-up" economics as an alternative to the traditional "trickle-down" theory . He explains that trickle-down, favored by past leaders like Park Chung-hee, focused on supporting large corporations (chaebols) with the expectation that wealth would eventually reach smaller businesses and the general public . While this approach might have been suitable for a developing nation, he argues it is no longer effective .

Instead, he proposes directly distributing wealth to the common people, particularly small business owners and the impoverished . He illustrates this with an example: if 250 trillion won is distributed to citizens, this money will flow into local markets, revitalizing small and medium-sized enterprises, and ultimately stimulating the entire economy from the bottom up . This "trickle-up" approach, he contends, is a more natural and effective way to foster economic growth and ensure widespread prosperity .


## The Three Ways of Seeing and the Cycle of Existence
Huh Kyung young outlines three ways of perceiving the world: through the physical eye (육안, <em>yugan</em>), through the ear (이문, <em>imun</em>), and through spiritual insight (영안, <em>yeong-an</em>) . He argues that relying solely on physical sight or hearing leads to a superficial understanding of reality, often resulting in deception and suffering . He emphasizes that true understanding comes from "contemplation" (관조, <em>gwanjo</em>), which involves seeing things as they truly are, beyond illusions and fleeting appearances .

He connects this to the Buddhist concept of "all phenomena are like dreams, illusions, bubbles, and shadows" (일체유위법 여몽환포영, <em>ilcheyuwibeop yeomonghwanpo-yeong</em>), suggesting that our physical existence is transient and illusory . He asserts that spiritual insight, or <em>yeong-an</em>, allows one to perceive the deeper truths and cycles of existence, transcending the limitations of physical perception . This higher form of insight enables one to understand the interconnectedness of all things and the true purpose of life .


## The Factory of Life and National Responsibility
Huh Kyung young uses the metaphor of a "factory" to describe the nation and its citizens . He states that if the "factory" (the nation) is not producing "products" (children), it will eventually fail . He criticizes the current state where the national "factory" is producing "defective products" (problematic youth) due to societal issues and a lack of proper guidance . He warns that if this trend continues, the nation will face collapse .

He also touches upon the concept of "ancestral lineage" (한민족, <em>hanminjok</em>), asserting that the Korean people possess a unique spiritual heritage and a special "tongue" (입성, <em>ipseong</em>) that allows them to pronounce certain sounds that other nations cannot . He believes this signifies a divine mission for the Korean people to lead the world . He concludes by reiterating that the nation's problems, including low birth rates and societal dysfunction, are not merely economic but spiritual, requiring a profound shift in consciousness and governance .
