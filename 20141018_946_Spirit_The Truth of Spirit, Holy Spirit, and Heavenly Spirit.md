## The Truth of Spirit, Holy Spirit, and Heavenly Spirit


## Date: 20141018


## Keywords: Huh Kyung young, Spirit, Holy Spirit, Heavenly Spirit, Capitalism


## Content


## The Pitfalls of the 40s and the Wisdom of Age
Huh Kyung young discusses the challenges faced by individuals in their 40s, contrasting them with the wisdom gained in old age. He points out that figures like Nelson Mandela became president in their 80s, after having challenged for the presidency in their 40s . The 40s are often characterized as a period of "불혹" (bulhok), meaning a lack of doubt or uncertainty about the world . However, Huh Kyung young argues that this period lacks "fermentation" or maturity, which is crucial for true understanding .

He draws a parallel with Moses, who possessed all the qualities of a king at 40—education, martial arts skills, good looks, and a noble lineage . Yet, Heaven did not allow Moses to become king at that age. Instead, Moses was forced to flee to the Midian desert after killing an Egyptian who was harming a fellow Israelite . For 40 years, Moses lived as a shepherd, contemplating the stars and praying to God . During this time, God revealed that if Moses were to become king at 40, he would serve God based on his own will, but if he became a leader at 80, he would serve God according to Heaven's will .

Huh Kyung young emphasizes that a 40-year-old, believing in their own capabilities, would likely act impulsively, leading to endeavors akin to the Tower of Babel . He advises that individuals in their 40s, especially entrepreneurs, should acknowledge that even their best decisions represent only 10% of a truly good idea, with 90% remaining as blind spots . Overconfidence and a refusal to seek counsel from elders or experts like Huh Kyung young can lead to utter failure . He stresses that the 40s are a dangerous period because one feels knowledgeable, yet lacks true wisdom .

This principle extends to religious leaders. A 40-year-old pastor, no matter how fervently they preach, is considered "fake" by Huh Kyung young . True spiritual leadership emerges when one reaches 80, having shed worldly desires for power, wealth, and youth . Young pastors, therefore, should continuously study, endure hardship, and avoid seeking comfort . They should embrace challenging environments, like remote villages or missionary work in Africa, rather than pursuing easy paths or financial gain . For business leaders, this means investing in learning and seeking knowledge, rather than indulging in leisure after earning money . Those who fail to do so will find themselves with nothing by their 50s or 60s .

Heaven, according to Huh Kyung young, often "kills" a person before truly using them, akin to a golfer learning to relax their body to improve their swing . To become a true pastor or monk, one must "die" to their ego and be reborn, seeking hardship rather than comfort . Monks, for instance, should experience earning a living to truly appreciate the offerings of their devotees .


## The True Nature of Money and Power
Huh Kyung young challenges the common perception of money as merely a means to satisfy needs . Instead, he defines money as "power that can be held in one's hand, given by Heaven" . This tangible power is unique to money . Misusing this power can lead to ruin, as money, in the form of currency, bestows authority upon its holder . Giving large sums of money to an undeserving child, for example, can lead them to abuse this power, causing harm to others . He illustrates this with an example of a wealthy 40-year-old who uses their money to exploit relationships, leading to the downfall of others .

He further explains that even a president, who wields immense power, ultimately controls the national budget—hundreds of trillions of won . Similarly, a city mayor's power stems from their control over the city's budget . Thus, those who hold power are those who control money .

Huh Kyung young asserts that power, whether held by political or religious leaders, is divinely ordained . He outlines a hierarchy of influence:
1. **Power (권력)**: Directly connected to Heaven and destiny (운) .
2. **Law (법)**: Subservient to power, often influenced by the wealthy .
3. **Reason/Logic (이치)**: Governed by law, typically the domain of academics and learned individuals .
4. **Emotion/Sentiment (정)**: Governed by reason, characteristic of common people who act on courage and loyalty .

He uses the example of Hong Kong to illustrate the extreme consequences of unchecked capitalism . The exorbitant cost of living, with apartments costing billions of won and monthly rents for shops in the tens of millions, has created a society where 90% of the population suffers, while a mere 10% live in luxury . This disparity leads to social unrest, as seen in the protests where many participants are young people unable to afford rent .

Huh Kyung young warns that if capitalism is not transformed into "capital-middle-class-ism" (자본 중산주의), where capitalists are respected but the middle class constitutes 90% of society, the world will face widespread riots . His goal is to create a system where corporate profits cycle back to the common people, ensuring that wealth benefits the many, not just the few . He laments that despite a national budget of 370 trillion won, Korea's middle class has shrunk from 40% to 20%, with 75% now considered common people and only 5% wealthy . The rapid accumulation of wealth by the rich, at a rate unimaginable to ordinary workers, exacerbates this problem . He criticizes politicians for exacerbating this issue, predicting that future generations will suffer from widespread poverty and social upheaval . He foresees a future where 99.9% of the world's population will be impoverished, with only 1% surviving .


## The Three Spirits: Simnyeong, Seongnyeong, and Cheonnyeong
Huh Kyung young introduces three distinct types of spiritual energy: Simnyeong (심령), Seongnyeong (성령), and Cheonnyeong (천령) .

1. **Simnyeong (Spirit of the Mind)**: This is the spirit residing within every human mind . It is linked to one's true nature (진성), which is described in the Samil Singo (삼일신고) . This true nature is further divided into three aspects, known as Samjin (삼진):
  - **Jinsung (진성)**: The mind (마음), which can be good (선) or evil (악), leading to blessings (복) or misfortune (화) .
  - **Jinmyeong (진명)**: Life force or 'Gi' (기), the electrical energy flowing through the body . It can be clear (맑음) or cloudy (흐림), influencing longevity (장수) or premature death (단명) . Clear 'Gi' is associated with high atmospheric pressure, while cloudy 'Gi' is linked to low pressure and stress, affecting blood circulation .
  - **Jinjeong (진정)**: The physical body (육체), which can be thick (두터움) or thin (얇음) .

    Huh Kyung young emphasizes that the 'Gi' (life force) is directly tied to one's physical well-being and sustenance. Without food, one's 'Gi' diminishes, regardless of meditation practices . He also clarifies the meaning of 'Myeong' (명), which signifies "Heaven's command" or "destiny," often represented by a banner (영) carried before a king . This 'Myeong' is the life given by Heaven to sustain existence .

    He also corrects a common linguistic error, explaining that using "우리" (uri, "our") for personal possessions like "our wife" or "our father" is incorrect, as "우리" implies a shared, communal entity, akin to God . One should say "my wife" or "my father" for personal relationships .

2. **Seongnyeong (Holy Spirit)**: This is the spirit that enters anyone who diligently believes in God, as taught in churches and temples . However, Huh Kyung young states that the Holy Spirit does not cause physical transformation . He contrasts this with the teachings of Christianity, where self-negation (denying oneself) leads to affirmation and blessings, mirroring Jesus's path of suffering and death to achieve salvation . He criticizes those who boast about their religious devotion, stating that true faith involves humility and acknowledging one's shortcomings before God . Buddhism, on the other hand, emphasizes self-affirmation, where the individual is seen as the universe itself ("천상천하 유아독존" - "In heaven and on earth, I alone am the honored one") .

3. **Cheonnyeong (Heavenly Spirit)**: This is a unique spiritual energy that only enters those who look at or call upon Huh Kyung young . It is a direct, transformative power from Heaven, capable of healing illnesses and changing one's physical state . Huh Kyung young demonstrates this by performing a "power test" where individuals' physical strength dramatically increases when they look at him or call his name, and decreases when they call other names or look at other images . He asserts that this power is far more potent and accurate than any medical diagnostic tool . He claims that this Heavenly Spirit can even enter people through his photograph or by calling his name over the phone, regardless of distance . He states that this spirit protects individuals from diseases like Ebola, but requires continuous remembrance of his name . This Heavenly Spirit is the 'spirit' used by the Creator who made humanity, and it is only accessible when the Creator comes in human form . He claims that his presence brings about a cosmic shift, and that those who listen to his lectures are blessed .


## The Prophecy of the Messiah and Huh Kyung young's Role
Huh Kyung young references the "Wonhyo Gyeolsa" (원효결사), a text interpreted by Kim Joong-tae, which prophesies the coming of a Messiah to the Korean Peninsula . This Messiah, according to the text, will be a "True Man" (진인) who comes to save the world, embodying the roles of Maitreya Buddha, Jesus, and Christ . The text also states that this figure will have the surname "Kim" (김) and will be born in Gimhae (김해) . Huh Kyung young connects this to his own identity, stating that his surname is "Heo" (허) but that the text refers to him .

The prophecy describes the Messiah as someone who will come to Earth with a specific "Heavenly number" of 11, which is a fundamental principle of the universe . Huh Kyung young links this to the "magic square" (마방진), a numerical puzzle found in European cathedrals, which he claims contains the "Messiah Code" . He interprets the numbers within this magic square, specifically 11 and 14, as representing his own name and the "Alpha and Omega" .

The Wonhyo Gyeolsa also describes the chaotic period before the Messiah's arrival, characterized by numerous self-proclaimed leaders and ideologies, leading to national confusion . It mentions the influx of "doctors" from America and "generals" from China after liberation, highlighting the intellectual and political disarray . The prophecy also foretells that only the South (남쪽) will find a path to survival, while the North (북쪽) will face ruin, a prediction Huh Kyung young interprets as a divine decision to abandon the "unfilial child" (North Korea) . The Korean War (경인신묘년) is also described as a conflict between "brothers" .

Huh Kyung young asserts that he is the one who has come to fulfill these prophecies, bringing peace to the world . He claims that his appearance is not about performing grand miracles but about subtly demonstrating the Heavenly Spirit . He also reveals a numerical code within the magic square that indicates he will appear in Korea at the age of 66, a number that is double Jesus's age at his death (33) . This signifies his "fully ripened" state, in contrast to Jesus's "unripened" state . He states that he will begin teaching his truths at 66 and will remain on Earth for 396 years, a period during which those who call his name can live for 365 years .

He warns that those who criticize or mock him will face dire consequences, including illness and the downfall of their families . He emphasizes that his presence is a divine intervention, and that the universe was not created by chance, as evidenced by the unique power associated with his name . He claims that his name can transform cells and even change the properties of milk and water .


## The Path to Spiritual Cultivation
Huh Kyung young outlines a three-step process for spiritual cultivation, derived from the Samil Singo, which he contrasts with the 108 afflictions (번뇌) of Buddhism :

1. **Jigam (지감 - Restrain Sensations)**: This involves controlling one's senses and emotions, such as anger, fear, desire, and aversion . By restraining these sensations, one can improve their cellular health and avoid suffering from insomnia .
2. **Josik (조식 - Regulate Breathing)**: This refers to practicing regulated breathing, like Dantian breathing, to calm the mind and eliminate negative energies from the body .
3. **Geumchok (금촉 - Forbid Touch)**: This means abstaining from inappropriate physical contact and sensual pleasures, such as those found in entertainment venues .

He asserts that these principles, found in the Samil Singo, are more detailed than even the Buddhist Tripitaka . He emphasizes that the Korean people, as a nation destined to lead the world, must not be swayed by their senses .


## Karma, Retribution, and Social Responsibility
Huh Kyung young discusses the concepts of karma (업) and retribution (보), particularly in the context of collective suffering . He argues that the 332 victims of the Sewol Ferry disaster did not die due to their individual karma, but rather due to the karma of others, such as the ferry captain, government officials who failed to rescue them, and figures like Yoo Byung-eun . These victims, he explains, receive "retribution" (보) in the form of compensation, which the perpetrators must eventually repay .

He warns that if the wealthy (the 1%) continue to accumulate wealth at the expense of the poor (the 99%), it will inevitably lead to revolutions and violence, as seen in the French Revolution and the Korean War . He cites historical examples where wealthy landowners who were benevolent were spared during times of upheaval, while those who exploited others were killed . This illustrates that one's actions towards others create either positive or negative "retribution" that can affect their descendants .

Huh Kyung young stresses that all individuals are interconnected, and the suffering of one can impact the well-being of others . For instance, if many people lose sleep watching a baseball game, a sleep-deprived driver could cause a fatal accident involving someone else's child . Therefore, he concludes, it is imperative to help the poor, as their suffering can ultimately lead to the downfall of the wealthy . Even those who believe they have committed no wrong can suffer due to the collective karma of society, as exemplified by the French monarchy .


## The All-Seeing Eye of Heaven
Huh Kyung young concludes by stating that human eyes are not merely "windows to the soul" but "Heaven's hidden cameras" . Through human and animal eyes, God, the Creator, observes and monitors everything in the universe, leaving no place to hide . This omnipresent observation underscores the importance of one's actions and thoughts, as they are constantly being recorded by the divine.
