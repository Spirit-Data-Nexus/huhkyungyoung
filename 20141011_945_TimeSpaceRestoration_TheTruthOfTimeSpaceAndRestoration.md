## The Truth of Time, Space, and Restoration


## Date: 2014-10-11


## Keywords: Time, Space, Restoration, Capitalism, Korean Classics


## Content


## The Emptiness of Time and Space and the Brain as a Black Box
Huh Kyung young began his lecture by stating that both space and time are ultimately empty, or "gong" (공) . He explained that while one might spend a significant amount of time listening to his lectures, the temporal aspect of this experience is ultimately void . However, he posited that even if the content of these lectures is not consciously retained, a hypnotist could elicit all the information from one's mind . This phenomenon, he noted, is remarkable because even individuals who struggle with literacy can articulate the lecture's content in detail .

Huh Kyung young attributed this to the brain's function as a recording device, transcending the limitations of time and space . He asserted that past actions, even those from 50 or 5,000 years ago, remain recorded in the brain and can be recalled under hypnosis . This highlights the immense capacity of the brain, which he claimed humans utilize only about 1% of . The remaining 99% holds the accumulated experiences of billions of years, making the brain a "black box" containing the entire history of an individual's existence .


## The Three Great Classics of the Korean People
Huh Kyung young then introduced the three great classics of the Korean people: the Cheonbugyeong, Samil Singo, and Chamjeon Gyegyeong . He lamented that many Koreans have forgotten these foundational texts, instead focusing solely on the Bible or Buddhist scriptures . He explained that the Cheonbugyeong is akin to the Book of Genesis in Christianity, detailing the creation of humans, stars, and the universe, and prophesying the arrival of a leader who will guide this cosmic harmony . This text, he referred to as the "Hwahwagyung" (Book of Harmony) .

The Samil Singo, he stated, is a "Kyohwagyung" (Book of Teaching), imparting fundamental truths . The Chamjeon Gyegyeong, on the other hand, is a "Chihwagyung" (Book of Governance), which teaches how to govern the world . Huh Kyung young emphasized that the Chamjeon Gyegyeong contains all the wisdom found in the Tripitaka Koreana (Palgwan Daejanggyeong) and predates it, with the Cheonbugyeong being even older, originating before the Goguryeo era and the arrival of Buddhism . He criticized the historical suppression of these texts, particularly by Japan, which burned Korean history books and created a new narrative . He asserted that these classics demonstrate Korea's status as a cultural superpower with a history spanning 10,000 years .


## Critique of Capitalism and the Role of the Chihwagyung
Huh Kyung young then launched into a critique of contemporary capitalism, identifying its primary problem as the widening gap between capital owners and laborers . He illustrated this by noting that the wealth of capital owners increases at a rate 100 times faster than that of laborers, leading to 1% of the population controlling 99.9% of the world's wealth . This disparity, he argued, leads to a cycle where the middle class descends into poverty and eventually homelessness, with the wealthy accumulating wealth at an unrealistic pace while ordinary people struggle .

He presented the Chamjeon Gyegyeong as a text that already predicted these societal issues and the need for a "Messiah" to guide the world . He argued that direct divine intervention is necessary to govern society, as human-led systems inevitably lead to ruin . Huh Kyung young cited Franklin D. Roosevelt's New Deal policies as an example of a leader who understood the "secret of capitalism" and implemented measures to reduce the wealth gap, such as an 80% income tax on the rich and direct aid to the poor . He contrasted this with Ronald Reagan's policies, which he claimed exacerbated wealth inequality despite Reagan's popularity . Huh Kyung young asserted that his own political vision, rooted in the Chihwagyung, aims to rectify these imbalances by ensuring that the benefits of economic growth are shared more equitably, rather than solely enriching the wealthy . He also criticized policies that cut public servant pensions while reducing taxes for the wealthy, arguing that such measures harm the economy by concentrating wealth rather than circulating it .


## The Concept of Hwasinbul (Incarnate Buddha) and the Importance of Perspective
Huh Kyung young delved into the Buddhist concept of Hwasinbul (Incarnate Buddha), explaining that it is not about a literal Buddha appearing, but rather about how individuals perceive others . He used the example of the monk Muhak Daesa seeing the murderous King Taejo Yi Seong-gye as a Buddha, illustrating that one's perspective transforms the perceived reality . Similarly, he noted that a daughter might see her bank robber father as a protective Buddha, while the victims of the robbery would see him as a criminal or demon .

This concept, he explained, is rooted in the idea of "Ilche Yushimjo" (一切唯心造), meaning "everything is created by the mind," a core teaching of Shakyamuni Buddha . He emphasized that whether someone is seen as a Buddha, a demon, or a savior depends entirely on the observer's mind . Huh Kyung young extended this to the idea that all people are simultaneously angels and devils, and it is our perception that determines which aspect we see . He also applied this to the concept of "Hwasinbul" in the context of one's immediate family, stating that one's spouse or parents are the most immediate "gods" or "Buddhas" to be revered .


## The Eight Actions (Haengju Jwa-wa Eomuk Dongjeong) and Restoration
Huh Kyung young introduced the "eight actions" (행주좌와 어묵동정 - Haengju Jwa-wa Eomuk Dongjeong), which encompass all human activities: walking, standing, sitting, lying down, speaking, remaining silent, moving, and being still . He asserted that the outcome of these actions determines one's "restoration power" (복원력), the ability to recover from setbacks . He used the Sewol Ferry disaster as a tragic example of what happens when this balance is lost . The captain, by removing the ballast water and overloading the cargo, disregarded the principle of maintaining balance, leading to the ship's capsizing .

He stressed that individuals must maintain their "balance water" in all eight actions to ensure they can recover from any misfortune . He linked this to the idea that one's present actions determine their future, just as past actions determine the present . He criticized those who engage in unethical behavior while expecting a bright future, comparing them to the captain who removed the ballast water for financial gain, ultimately leading to disaster .


## The Significance of Time and Place
Huh Kyung young highlighted the critical importance of "time" (시) and "place" (지) in all endeavors, drawing from the Samil Singo . He explained that just as the time of birth can influence a child's appearance, the timing of actions, such as starting or ending a business, is crucial . Similarly, the "place" or "land" where an activity occurs is equally vital . He warned that disregarding the right time and place, or "Gaebul-i-si" (개불이시) and "Balbul-i-ji" (발불이지), leads to chaos and the subversion of human morality . He connected this to the current state of the world, where humanity's exploitation of the Earth (place) and disregard for proper timing have led to environmental degradation and societal disorder .

He further illustrated the importance of time and place through the example of the competition between food seasoning companies like Miwon, Samsung's Dasida, and LG's Green . He explained how Samsung successfully overcame Miwon's market dominance by introducing Dasida at the right time, effectively changing the "name" of the product and upgrading it . He emphasized that in business, one must always innovate and introduce new products or ideas rather than merely imitating competitors, as timing is paramount . He suggested that Samsung, facing competition in the smartphone market, should similarly innovate with new features like instant translation or memory enhancement to stay ahead .


## The Purpose of Religion, Philosophy, Faith, and Politics
Huh Kyung young asserted that religion, philosophy, faith, and politics are not ends in themselves but merely "means" (수단) for human existence . He challenged the audience to identify the ultimate "purpose" (목적) behind these endeavors . He revealed that the true purpose is "filial piety" (효도) – honoring one's parents and, by extension, the divine creator and ancestors . He argued that this vertical lineage, from the creator to parents, is the fundamental axis of human existence, with religion and faith serving as branches or means to fulfill this primary duty .

He criticized religious leaders who emphasize God or Buddha while neglecting filial piety, stating that true devotion begins with honoring one's parents . He extended this concept to include caring for all elders, emphasizing that filial piety is an unending duty that extends beyond one's immediate family . He concluded that without this underlying purpose of filial piety, all other human endeavors, including religion and politics, lose their meaning .


## The Principle of Sincerity (Seongsin-aeje) in Chamjeon Gyegyeong
Huh Kyung young introduced the "Eight Principles" (팔리훈 - Pallihun) from the Chamjeon Gyegyeong, which include sincerity (성 - seong), trust (신 - sin), love (애 - ae), and temperance (제 - je) . He focused on "sincerity," defining it as the alignment of words and actions . He argued that without sincerity, actions lead to disaster (화 - hwa), while sincerity brings blessings (복 - bok) . He again referenced the Sewol Ferry disaster, stating that the lack of sincerity and the presence of deceit in the actions of those responsible led to the catastrophe .

He emphasized that true prayer and devotion require sincerity, where one's words and actions are in harmony . He reiterated that the Chamjeon Gyegyeong provides the fundamental principles for governing society, emphasizing that political policies, like his own proposals for supporting families, must be rooted in sincerity . He criticized politicians who lack sincerity, prioritizing personal gain or special interests over the well-being of the people . He concluded by reminding the audience that the "god" in one's home, one's spouse or family, is the most important deity, and true spiritual practice begins with honoring them .
