## The Truth of Admonition and Moral Rectification
Huh Kyung young introduces the concept of "Banmyeon-gyosa" (반면교사), which translates to learning from the failures or negative examples of others . He emphasizes that observing the mistakes of others should lead to self-reflection and moral improvement. For instance, he cites the example of a city council member who accumulated wealth through illicit means, never using it for good, and whose sole ambition was to become a "hotel king" . Such an individual, upon death, leaves all their accumulated wealth behind, having made no "exchange" (환전) for good deeds in the afterlife . This serves as a negative example, teaching us to avoid such a path .

Huh Kyung young further explains that "Banmyeon-gyosa" is intrinsically linked with "Gaegwacheonseon" (개과천선), meaning to turn over a new leaf and reform oneself . The idea is to learn from negative examples and transform one's actions towards goodness . However, he notes that people often fail to do this, being drawn to their vices, like gambling or real estate speculation, to the exclusion of human values . He uses the analogy of "choknokja bulgyeonsan" (축록자불견산), meaning "one who chases a deer cannot see the mountain," to illustrate how people become so fixated on their pursuits (e.g., money, property) that they lose sight of what truly matters: people and ethical conduct . He argues that true wealth involves not only accumulating money but also using it for benevolent purposes, such as helping those in need .


## Lessons from the Sewol Ferry Disaster and Political Corruption
Huh Kyung young applies the principle of "Banmyeon-gyosa" to contemporary issues, particularly the Sewol Ferry disaster . He criticizes the authorities for instructing students to remain inside the sinking ship, calling it a "blatant lie" . He highlights the tragedy of students who died believing they would be rescued, trusting the government and the nation . This event, he asserts, should serve as a profound lesson for society .

He extends his critique to the political system, advocating for the abolition of local council systems and local government councils, which he views as breeding grounds for corruption . He claims that these systems lead to significant national budget deficits and foster "thieves" . He contrasts this with a system where the president appoints city and provincial governors, which he believes would drastically reduce wasteful spending and debt . He alleges that a substantial portion of local government budgets, such as the 13 trillion won debt of Incheon City, is siphoned off into the pockets of corrupt officials .

Huh Kyung young also criticizes the number of National Assembly members, suggesting that 100 members would suffice, similar to the U.S. Senate, rather than the current 300 . He questions why National Assembly members receive salaries when they also accept significant political donations, arguing that this practice encourages corruption . He points out that while there are limits on reported donations, unofficial funds are often exchanged . He argues that this system allows politicians to prioritize fundraising over their duties, leading to a "rotten" political landscape .

He further criticizes the current system where local government officials, often from the same political party, collude to approve unnecessary projects, such as roads with low traffic, which then incur further costs to the national budget due to contractual agreements . He states that 70% of the national budget is wasted on such unnecessary expenditures .


## Presidential System vs. Cabinet System
Huh Kyung young strongly advocates for a robust presidential system in South Korea, arguing against any form of cabinet system . He believes that a cabinet system, where a prime minister holds significant power, would lead to national ruin, citing historical examples where powerful secondary figures undermined the monarchy . He asserts that a strong prime minister in Korea would inevitably challenge the president, leading to instability . He uses an analogy of a household, stating that if both husband and wife are too strong, the family will collapse . Given North Korea's strong leadership, South Korea needs an equally strong presidential system for effective deterrence .

He criticizes any politician who advocates for a cabinet system in Korea, labeling them as "traitors" (내국노예) who seek to exploit power for personal gain . He argues that National Assembly members should serve as a check on power, not as centers of power themselves, as this leads to corruption and misuse of national funds . He contrasts Korea with Japan, which has a stable cabinet system due to the unifying presence of the Emperor as the head of state . In Korea, the President serves as both head of state and head of government, making a strong prime minister incompatible with the constitutional structure . He concludes that the prime minister's role is primarily to assist the president, and any attempt to elevate the prime minister to a "responsible prime minister" would signify the nation's downfall .

Huh Kyung young proposes establishing an imperial system in Korea, with himself as the Emperor, to provide a stable "center of gravity" (구심점) for the nation . He believes that only an Emperor, serving for life, can truly transform society, unlike a five-year presidential term . In such a system, a prime minister would serve under the Emperor, taking responsibility for policies, similar to Japan or Thailand .


## Critique of the Kim Young-ran Act and Legislative Flaws
Huh Kyung young criticizes the "Kim Young-ran Act" (김영란법), officially known as the Act on the Prohibition of Improper Solicitation and Receipt of Money and Valuables, for its flaws . He notes that the National Assembly repeatedly delayed its passage because it would primarily implicate politicians themselves . He argues that the law, while intended to prevent improper solicitations and conflicts of interest for public officials, was manipulated by politicians to create loopholes .

He points out that the existing bribery law (뇌물죄) requires proof of a direct benefit to the recipient, making it difficult to prosecute cases where gifts are given without explicit quid pro quo . The Kim Young-ran Act aimed to address this by prohibiting even gifts without a clear understanding, but Huh Kyung young argues that the politicians distorted its original intent .

His primary critique is that the law, as modified by the National Assembly, punishes both the giver and receiver of improper solicitations . He argues that to truly eradicate corruption, only the recipient should be punished, while the giver should be incentivized to report the act . He proposes a system where the giver is not punished and receives a full refund of the money given if they report the corruption, regardless of when the report is made . This, he believes, would make public officials hesitant to accept bribes, as they could be reported at any time . He asserts that the current law, by punishing both parties, encourages them to remain silent, thus perpetuating corruption .


## The Parable of the Magpie and Crow: Misguided Perceptions
Huh Kyung young uses the parable of the magpie (까치) and the crow (까마귀) to illustrate how human perceptions often invert truth . He explains that while magpies are often seen as symbols of good fortune, they are actually harmful to humans, destroying crops and causing over 1 trillion won in agricultural damage annually . Magpies are omnivores that scavenge for food, and their calls, often interpreted as heralding guests, are merely a sign of their opportunistic nature .

In contrast, crows, often perceived as ominous, are actually beneficial . Crows consume insects that are harmful to humans, such as mosquitoes, and do not scavenge human food . He also mentions the concept of "Banpojihyo" (반포지효), or filial piety of the crow, where young crows feed their elderly parents, symbolizing the highest form of filial devotion . Crows are also believed to warn humans of impending danger . Huh Kyung young laments that people often spit at crows, considering them bad luck, while welcoming magpies, demonstrating a profound misunderstanding of their true nature . He likens corrupt politicians to magpies, who are perceived as good but are actually detrimental to the nation .


## The Importance of Divine Wisdom and Human Potential
Huh Kyung young discusses the concept of "unsu" (운수), which refers to one's fortune or destiny . He illustrates this with an analysis of former President Park Geun-hye's name, assigning numerical values to characters to predict her life stages, including her early success, mid-life struggles, and eventual rise to the presidency . He asserts that these numerical predictions are accurate, suggesting that human destiny is largely predetermined .

However, he also states that while 99% of destiny is fixed, 1% can be changed through conscious effort and "spiritual awakening" (정신 차리고 있어야) . This 1% represents the ability to make choices that align with a higher purpose, such as using wealth for good deeds rather than selfish pursuits . He criticizes young people who seek wealth without a clear, benevolent purpose, likening them to "tyrants with atomic bombs" .

He distinguishes between "knowledge" (지식), which is accumulated by humans, and "wisdom" (지혜), which is divinely bestowed . He argues that humans have forgotten this innate divine wisdom, and his purpose is to revive it . He uses the example of a fetus in the womb, which instinctively knows when and how to be born, demonstrating an inherent wisdom that is later lost . This innate wisdom allows for communication and understanding beyond conventional means, which humans have forgotten .

He further emphasizes the immense complexity and value of the human body, noting that it comprises 100 trillion cells, 4,600 trillion DNA chromosomes, and 9,200 "yang" (a vast numerical unit) of components . He argues that doctors, who treat this incredibly complex system, deserve their high compensation, but even they only address a small fraction of the body's components . He asserts that every human being, regardless of their social status, possesses inherent divine value, akin to the essential organs of the body .


## The Importance of Trust and Moral Leadership
Huh Kyung young highlights the critical role of "trust" (신뢰) and "morality" (도덕성) in leadership . He argues that former President Park Geun-hye was elected primarily due to the public's trust in her moral integrity, believing she would not engage in corruption or prioritize personal gain . However, he contends that this trust was eroded by events like the Sewol Ferry disaster and controversial appointments of morally questionable officials . He criticizes the lack of thorough vetting for ministerial candidates, leading to the selection of individuals with histories of misconduct .

He also references the scandal involving a presidential spokesperson accused of sexual harassment during an overseas trip, which further damaged the president's moral standing . He asserts that while the president's personal morality may not have changed, the actions of her subordinates and the perceived lack of accountability undermined public trust . He concludes that a leader's identity is tied to their moral integrity, and any compromise on this front jeopardizes their ability to govern effectively .


## The True Meaning of "Chiljeon Palgi"
Huh Kyung young clarifies the true meaning of the idiom "Chiljeon Palgi" (칠전팔기), often misinterpreted as "seven battles, eight victories" . He explains that the character "전" (전) in this context means "to fall" or "to stumble" (자빠질 전차), not "to fight" (싸울 전차) . Therefore, "Chiljeon Palgi" correctly means "to fall seven times, but rise the eighth time" . He uses this to critique the current government, suggesting that it has repeatedly stumbled (칠전) and is failing to rise (탈도), instead continuing to fall . He emphasizes that after seven failures, one must learn from them and rise, rather than continuing to fall . He cites the example of boxer Hong Soo-hwan, who famously "fell seven times and rose the eighth," demonstrating resilience . He urges the government to embody this spirit for the benefit of the nation .


## The Purpose of the Appendix (맹장)
Huh Kyung young discusses the appendix (맹장), often considered a vestigial organ . He explains that the appendix plays a crucial role in regulating gravity and detecting the state of food in the intestines, communicating this information to the brain . He asserts that this organ is particularly sensitive during flight or in zero-gravity environments, making it essential for pilots and astronauts . He claims that individuals without an appendix would struggle to maintain orientation and judgment in such conditions, potentially leading to accidents . This, he concludes, demonstrates that every part of the human body, and indeed every human being, has a purpose and inherent value bestowed by a higher power .
