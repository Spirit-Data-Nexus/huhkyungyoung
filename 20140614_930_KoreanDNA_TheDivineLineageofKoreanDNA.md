## The Misconception of Korean Diligence 
There has been a misunderstanding regarding the Korean DNA, particularly concerning the notion of diligence. While some interpret remarks about Koreans as implying laziness, the original intent was to highlight their inherent industriousness. Historically, during the Joseon Dynasty, the social structure, with its system of landlords and tenant farmers, did not necessitate intense labor for survival, leading to a perception of idleness. However, in reality, Koreans are among the most diligent people globally.


## Korean Diligence and Global Recognition 
The industrious nature of Koreans is widely recognized. For instance, Jewish communities in other countries reportedly avoid Korean expatriates due to their relentless work ethic, often operating businesses without much rest. This diligence was notably demonstrated when President Park Chung-hee sent nurses and miners to Germany. Their exceptional work ethic earned Koreans a reputation in Germany and across Europe as a hardworking, compassionate, and self-sacrificing people. This positive image led to Germany providing significant aid to Korea. Many Korean female professors in Korea today are former nurses who pursued higher education while working in Germany, often studying late into the night.


## The True Meaning of Korean DNA 
The statement about Korean DNA being different from that of the Japanese and the need for the nation to "come to its senses" has been misinterpreted. The speaker, Huh Kyung young, clarifies that the true meaning is not that Koreans are inherently lazy, but that the Joseon Dynasty's social structure fostered a habit of idleness among an otherwise diligent people. This historical conditioning, he suggests, still lingers. The message is a call for the nation to move forward powerfully, not an accusation of current laziness. Misinterpreting such statements can lead to unfairly criticizing individuals.


## Critiques of Political Appointments and National Interest 
Huh Kyung young criticizes the tendency to excessively scrutinize political appointments, such as a prime ministerial nominee, when there are no significant issues. He argues that such actions hinder national progress and that a degree of acceptance is necessary for the country's benefit.


## The Origin of Korean DNA: A Divine Lineage 
Huh Kyung young emphasizes the importance of understanding the origin of Korean DNA, a topic he has not fully lectured on before. He delves into a unique interpretation of human creation, distinct from conventional religious teachings.


## The Nephilim and Early Human Creation 
According to Huh Kyung young, the first humans, referred to as Nephilim, were created by God 350 million years ago. This predates the common Christian belief that humanity began with Adam 6,000 years ago, a misconception he attributes to misinterpretations of the Bible by religious leaders.


## Reinterpreting Genesis: Adam, Cain, and the Nephilim 
Huh Kyung young offers a reinterpretation of the Genesis narrative. While the Bible states Adam was the first man, it also mentions Cain and Abel as his sons. He questions the origin of Cain's wife after Cain was cast out of Eden, a point often overlooked or unexplained in traditional Christian theology. He asserts that Adam's wife was Eve, created from Adam's rib. He humorously suggests that if Adam had lost more ribs, he would have had more wives.

He explains that Cain's offering was of crops (sweat), while Abel's was of meat (blood), signifying that blood sacrifices are favored by heaven. When Cain killed Abel, God cursed Cain, stating that anyone who found him would kill him. This implies the existence of other humans outside of Adam's direct lineage. After being expelled from Eden, Cain went to the "Land of Nod," where he found a wife and had a son, Enoch. This, Huh Kyung young explains, indicates that there were already numerous people living in the Land of Nod, created by God 350 million years ago, who were not descendants of Adam.


## Stages of Human Creation by God 
Huh Kyung young outlines a multi-stage process of human creation by God:

- **First Creation (350 million years ago):** The Nephilim. These early humans were adapted to a chaotic Earth, with smaller heads and larger bodies, resembling animals. They were not as intelligent as later creations.
- **Second Creation (2 million years ago):** Homo Erectus. These humans had slightly better intelligence than the first creation.
- **Third Creation (150,000 years ago):** Homo Sapiens. These humans also faced significant population reduction during ice ages.
- **Fourth Creation (150,000 years ago):** Neanderthal Man. These are identified as the ancestors of European Caucasians.
- **Fifth Creation (100,000 years ago):** Mitochondrial Eve (CHH Mitochondrial Eve).
- **Sixth Creation (10,000 years ago):** Adam. This Adam, according to Huh Kyung young, was created with the highest intelligence.

## The Korean and Jewish Connection 
Huh Kyung young asserts that the sixth creation, Adam, represents the ancestors of both the Jewish people and the Korean people. He points to the shared tradition of wearing a "gat" (a traditional Korean hat) by both Korean ancestors and Jewish men as evidence of their common lineage.


## The Significance of "Gat" and Shamanism 
The term "gat" is interpreted as signifying "God" or "divine." Huh Kyung young explains that shamans (mudang) historically wore "gat" during rituals to connect with the divine. He posits that Korean shamanism, or "musok," was the earliest and most direct form of communication with God, predating established religions like Christianity and Buddhism. He claims that ancient shamans could interact with figures like Mary, Jesus, and Buddha, possessing spiritual communication methods that modern religions lack. Western theological scholars, he notes, acknowledge the profound spiritual connection of ancient shamanic beliefs, often viewing modern religions as forced or artificial in comparison. This shared lineage with the Jewish people, he argues, explains the inherent intelligence of both groups.


## The Dispersion of Adam's Descendants and the "A" Prefix 
Huh Kyung young explains that the descendants of Adam, including the Chinese Han people and Europeans, spread across the globe. He notes that many places settled by Adam's descendants have names beginning with the "A" sound, such as Asadal (the first capital of Korea), Asia, America, and Africa. He also corrects the biblical "Garden of Eden" to "Garden of Aden," further emphasizing the "A" prefix.


## The Lineage of Jesus and Dangun 
Tracing the lineage from Adam, Huh Kyung young states that Noah was the 10th generation, Abraham the 20th, Boaz the 30th, Uzziah the 40th, Abiud the 50th, and Jesus the 60th. He clarifies that the 6,000-year timeline often cited in Christianity refers to 4,000 years before Jesus and 2,000 years after.

He then introduces Joktan, a great-grandson of Noah and the 15th generation from Adam. Joktan had 13 sons, the 12th of whom was named Haila. Huh Kyung young identifies Haila as Dangun, the legendary founder of Gojoseon, the first Korean kingdom. After Noah's flood, Joktan's descendants migrated, with some going to the Asian side, crossing the Tian Shan Mountains, and others heading towards Israel. This split, he explains, led to the divergence of the Jewish and Korean (Mongolian) peoples.


## Dangun as "Grandfather Haila" and the Mongolian Spot 
Huh Kyung young explains that Dangun is uniquely referred to as "Dangun Halabeoji" (Grandfather Dangun) in Korea because his original name was Haila, and "Haila Abeoji" (Father Haila) naturally evolved into "Halabeoji" (grandfather). He also equates Adam with Hwanung, the legendary divine being who descended from heaven in Korean mythology.

He further asserts that the descendants of Adam, including Koreans, possess a "Mongolian spot" on their buttocks. This birthmark, he claims, is a divine "seal" marking their lineage. He states that 99% of the world's population today are descendants of Mitochondrial Eve, created 100,000 years ago, while the descendants of Adam (Koreans and Jews) are a distinct, highly intelligent lineage.


## Reconciling Biblical Narratives with Pre-Adamic Humanity 
Huh Kyung young addresses the theological challenge of Cain finding a wife immediately after being expelled from Eden. He argues that traditional biblical interpretations are flawed, leading people to believe that no humans existed before Adam. He clarifies that the Bible focuses specifically on the lineage of Adam, treating it as a particular family history among many existing human lineages on Earth. The "Garden of Eden" is described as a specific region where a superior "seed" (Adam's lineage) was cultivated.


## The Problem with Exclusive Genealogies 
He criticizes the narrow perspective of some religious believers who only acknowledge Adam's genealogy, ignoring the existence of other human lineages. He emphasizes that the Korean people are descendants of Adam's lineage, and from this lineage, a Messiah will emerge in the Korean Peninsula.


## Debunking Misinterpretations of Cain's Wife 
Huh Kyung young refutes the common Christian explanation that Cain married a sister born to Adam and Eve after Cain's expulsion. He points to Genesis 4, which states that Cain went to the Land of Nod and found a wife there, implying the existence of other people (Nephilim) in that land. He cites biblical passages where God protects Cain from being killed by "anyone who finds him," further supporting the idea of pre-Adamic populations.


## The Shared DNA of Koreans and Japanese 
Huh Kyung young asserts that the Japanese people share the same DNA as Koreans, having migrated from Gaya and Silla in Korea. He states that Japan has historically adopted Korean culture and elements, confirming their shared ethnic background.


## The "Dure" Spirit and Korean Social Structure 
He discusses the "Dure" spirit, a communal farming tradition in Korea where people helped each other with agricultural tasks. This spirit, while fostering community, also discouraged individual ambition for wealth, leading to a collective, rather than individualistic, approach to prosperity. This, he suggests, contributed to a perceived lack of individual drive during the Joseon period.


## Korea's Geopolitical Significance: The "Elephant's Trunk" 
Huh Kyung young describes Korea's geographical position as crucial, likening it to an "elephant's trunk" or a "power outlet" connecting to the Asian continent. He explains that the Korean peninsula, particularly the southern regions like Geoje Island and Jindo, act as vital conduits for continental energy. He asserts that if this "outlet" were disconnected, Asia would collapse. This strategic importance, he claims, is why global powers like the US, China, Russia, and Japan are focused on controlling the Korean Peninsula. He states that he has come to Korea to unify the world, starting with Asia, and prevent global conflict.


## The Dangers of Premature Korean Unification 
Huh Kyung young warns against premature unification of North and South Korea, citing historical examples like Yemen and Vietnam, where unification led to conflict and communist takeover. He argues that the North, being geographically superior in terms of strength, would inevitably dominate the South.


## The North's Dominance and Historical Patterns 
He explains that in traditional Korean house layouts, the "anbang" (main room) is always in the north, signifying the place of elders and authority. This reflects a broader principle that northern regions or entities tend to be stronger than southern ones. He cites the American Civil War as an example, where the industrial North, despite initial financial disadvantages, ultimately defeated the wealthy, slave-owning South. He argues that the North's ideology, advocating for the abolition of slavery, was akin to communism.


## The Korean War and American Intervention 
Huh Kyung young emphasizes that without American intervention, particularly General MacArthur's Incheon Landing, South Korea would have fallen to the North. He asserts that the US played a crucial role in preserving South Korea's existence.

## The Flaw of the Prime Ministerial System in Korea 
Huh Kyung young argues that Korea's "feng shui" (geomancy) dictates that a strong prime minister leads to national disaster and revolution. He cites the example of Chang Myon's prime ministerial system, which he claims led to Park Chung-hee's coup. He asserts that a revolution occurs when the military intervenes during a national crisis, such as widespread public uprising against a corrupt government. However, if the military seizes power after a civilian revolution, it is a coup, not a revolution.


## The 4.19 Revolution vs. the 5.16 Coup 
He distinguishes between the 4.19 Student Revolution, which he considers a true revolution against Syngman Rhee's fraudulent election, and the 5.16 military takeover by Park Chung-hee, which he labels a coup. He argues that Park Chung-hee seized power after the students had already initiated the change, thus making it a coup rather than a revolution.


## The Dangers of a Strong Prime Minister 
Huh Kyung young reiterates his warning against appointing a strong prime minister in Korea, stating that it inevitably leads to conflict and instability. He believes that the current president's continued attempts to appoint a prime minister indicate a lack of understanding of Korea's unique "feng shui."


## Reinterpreting the Prime Ministerial Nominee's Remarks 
He defends the prime ministerial nominee, stating that his remarks about Korean DNA were not meant to be derogatory but rather to lament the wasted potential of the Korean people during the 500 years of the Joseon Dynasty, particularly due to the influence of Confucianism.


## The Negative Impact of Confucianism on Korean Culture 
Huh Kyung young criticizes Confucianism, defining it as a religion focused on ancestral rites and the worship of spirits, lacking a concept of an afterlife. He claims that Confucian scholars actively destroyed Buddhist statues and cultural heritage during the Joseon Dynasty. He also notes that early Christian missionaries in Korea faced persecution from Confucianists. He argues that while Confucianism had some positive aspects, its rigid social structure, including the system of slavery and low wages for servants, ultimately led to a decline in the Korean people's inherent diligence.


## Unification: War, Not a Jackpot 
He firmly states that Korean unification, if pursued prematurely, will lead to war, not prosperity. He advocates for a strategy where Korea first unifies Asia and then the world, before integrating North Korea, thereby preventing any potential betrayal or conflict from the North. He proposes "Unification is the Future" as a new slogan, emphasizing that the immediate focus should be on improving the lives of the Korean people.


## The Presidential System and the Role of the Prime Minister 
Huh Kyung young advocates for a strong presidential system, similar to that of the United States, where the president directly leads the government without a powerful prime minister. He argues that a strong prime minister in Korea's "feng shui" leads to the president's downfall and national instability. He cites the example of Chang Myon's prime ministerial system, which he claims was overthrown by Park Chung-hee. He also mentions Lee Hae-chan's attempt to assert prime ministerial authority against President Kim Young-sam, which resulted in his dismissal.


## The Danger of an Overly Capable Prime Minister 
He warns that appointing an overly capable prime minister in Korea can lead to the president being undermined or even overthrown. He cites the example of Choi Kyu-hah, who rose from a low-level civil servant to president after Park Chung-hee's assassination, as an instance where a strong prime minister could potentially usurp presidential power.
